# India — Domestic Education System

**Document purpose:** Reference knowledge so the GlobalyApp AI counsellor can interpret what an Indian student's stated education *means inside India* before any cross-country comparison is attempted.
**Research date:** 2026-08-21
**Scope:** Domestic system only.

> ## ⚠ THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT WARNING FOR INDIA
>
> **India is in the middle of the largest education restructuring in its history.** NEP 2020 replaced the `10+2+3` structure with `5+3+3+4`; the NHEQF (2022/23) and NCrF (2023) replaced the old degree/credit architecture; the NCF-SE (2023) is changing board examinations. **Implementation is staggered, uneven across states, and incomplete as of 2026.**
>
> This means **two Indian students of the same age can be in genuinely different systems.** The AI must always establish:
> 1. **Which board** issued the school qualification (CBSE / CISCE / NIOS / which state board)
> 2. **Which year** they completed it
> 3. **Whether their degree is a 3-year or 4-year bachelor's** (both are currently valid and both are being awarded)
>
> Never state a single "Indian system" answer without those three facts.

## 1.3 The structural reform: `10+2+3` → `5+3+3+4`

**The legacy structure (still the operative reality for most current applicants):**

```text
10 years of schooling (Classes 1–10)   → Class 10 board exam ("Secondary")
+ 2 years (Classes 11–12)              → Class 12 board exam ("Senior Secondary")
+ 3 years                              → Bachelor's degree
```
This is the famous **"10+2+3"** and it is why Indian students say **"10+2"** to mean "I finished school". ✅ (structure confirmed in existing Globaly knowledge base; "10+2" phrasing is universal)

**The NEP 2020 structure (`5+3+3+4`)** — NEP 2020 was approved by the Union Cabinet on **29 July 2020** ⚠:

| Stage | Grades | Ages | Duration | Character (as reported) |
|-------|--------|------|----------|-------------------------|
| **Foundational** | 3 years pre-school/Anganwadi + Grades 1–2 | **3–8** | 5 years | Play-based and activity-based learning |
| **Preparatory** | Grades 3–5 | **8–11** | 3 years | Introduction of more formal classroom learning |
| **Middle** | Grades 6–8 | **11–14** | 3 years | Experiential and discovery-based learning |
| **Secondary** | Grades 9–12 | **14–18** | 4 years | Multidisciplinary learning, subject flexibility |

⚠ Stage boundaries and ages from multiple corroborating secondary sources; the primary source is the **NEP 2020 document** at the Ministry of Education, which was not read in full in this pass.

> **Critical point the AI must not miss:** `5+3+3+4` **does not change the grade numbering**. Indian students still study Grades 1–12, still sit a **Class 10** board examination and a **Class 12** board examination. What changed is the **pedagogical staging**, the formal inclusion of **3 years of pre-school** in the school structure, and the treatment of Grades 9–12 as **one 4-year secondary stage** rather than "secondary (9–10) + senior secondary (11–12)".
>
> **The Class 10 and Class 12 board examinations remain the operative qualifications.** ✅

# 2. Education Levels — Actual Structure

```text
PRE-SCHOOL / ANGANWADI  (3 years, ages 3–6)   ── part of the Foundational Stage under NEP 2020
        ↓
FOUNDATIONAL STAGE      Grades 1–2   (ages 6–8)
        ↓
PREPARATORY STAGE       Grades 3–5   (ages 8–11)     ── NCrF Credit Level 1 attained at Grade 5
        ↓
MIDDLE STAGE            Grades 6–8   (ages 11–14)    ── NCrF Credit Level 2 attained at Grade 8
        ↓
SECONDARY STAGE         Grades 9–12  (ages 14–18)
        ├── Class 10 BOARD EXAMINATION  ── NCrF Credit Level 3
        └── Class 12 BOARD EXAMINATION  ── NCrF Credit Level 4  ← university-entry qualification
                ↓                                    ↘
        HIGHER EDUCATION (NHEQF 4.5–8)        VOCATIONAL (NSQF / NCrF 1–8)
        ├── UG Certificate    (1 yr,  40 cr, L4.5)   ├── ITI trades (DGT)
        ├── UG Diploma        (2 yr,  80 cr, L5)     ├── Polytechnic Diploma (3 yr)
        ├── Bachelor's        (3 yr, 120 cr, L5.5)   ├── B.Voc / M.Voc
        ├── Bachelor's Hons   (4 yr, 160 cr, L6)     └── NSQF skill certificates
        ├── PG Diploma        (1 yr,  40 cr, L6)
        ├── Master's          (1–2 yr, 40/80 cr, L6.5–7)
        └── Doctoral (Ph.D.)  (L8)
```

**Integrated programmes** ✅ (from NHEQF Table 1):
- **Integrated Bachelor's–Master's**: **five years (ten semesters)** after Grade 12.
- **4-year dual-major Integrated Teacher Education Programme (ITEP)** → B.A. B.Ed. / B.Sc. B.Ed. / B.Com. B.Ed., **160 credits**.
- **Integrated B.Ed.–M.Ed.**: three years (six semesters) after a bachelor's degree.
- **B.Ed.**: **two years** after a bachelor's degree, **or one year** after a 4-year Honours/Honours-with-Research bachelor's or a master's.
- **M.Ed.**: two years after a 2-year B.Ed. or after a 4-year dual-degree ITEP.

| **"Junior college"** | The institution delivering **Classes 11–12** in Maharashtra and some other states | ⚠ **Not higher education** — terminology trap |
| **"+2" / "10+2"** | Classes 11–12 / school completion | Universal Indian phrasing |
| **AISSE** | All India Secondary School Examination — **CBSE Class 10** | ⚠ |
| **AISSCE** | All India Senior School Certificate Examination — **CBSE Class 12** | ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) |
| **ICSE** | Indian Certificate of Secondary Education — **CISCE Class 10** | ⚠ |
| **ISC** | Indian School Certificate — **CISCE Class 12** | ⚠ |
| **B.A./B.Sc. (Hons)** — old style | A 3-year honours degree under the old system | ⚠ **Not the same as the NEP 4-year Honours degree.** A pre-NEP "(Hons)" is usually **3 years, 120 credits**; the NEP Honours is **4 years, 160 credits**. **Major trap.** |

> ⚠ **The "(Hons)" trap is India-specific and high-impact.** Under the old system, many Indian universities (notably Delhi University, Calcutta University) awarded **3-year B.A./B.Sc. (Honours)** degrees, where "Honours" denoted a *specialised single-subject* degree, not an extra year. Under NHEQF, **"Bachelor's Degree (Honours)" means a 4-year, 160-credit programme**. The AI must **ask the duration**, never infer it from the word "Honours".

## 6.1 Streams

Traditionally, Indian students choose a **stream** on entering Class 11:

| Stream | Typical subjects | Leads to |
|--------|-----------------|----------|
| **Science (PCM)** | Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics + English + one elective | Engineering, architecture, computing (JEE) |
| **Science (PCB)** | Physics, Chemistry, Biology + English + one elective | Medicine, allied health, life sciences (NEET) |
| **Science (PCMB)** | Physics, Chemistry, Maths **and** Biology | Keeps both engineering and medical routes open |
| **Commerce** | Accountancy, Business Studies, Economics, Maths/other | B.Com., BBA, CA, finance |
| **Humanities / Arts** | History, Political Science, Geography, Psychology, Sociology, languages | B.A., law, social sciences, civil services |
| **Vocational** | Board-specific vocational subject packages | B.Voc., employment, polytechnic |

⚠ Stream names and subject packages vary by board. The **PCM / PCB / PCMB** distinction is critical because it determines which entrance examinations the student is eligible for (§20).

**NEP/NCF-SE 2023 change:** NCF-SE 2023 advocates **doing away with rigid streams**, allowing integrated choice across arts, humanities and sciences, and proposes a **semester system for Classes 11–12** with **at least two languages, at least one native to India**. ⚠ (reported from NCF-SE 2023 coverage; released **23 August 2023**) [VOLATILE — implementation is board-by-board and incomplete]

> ⚠ **AI rule:** a student who says "I did Class 12 Science" has told you the stream but **not** whether it was **PCM or PCB** — and that difference decides which degree programmes they are eligible for. Always ask for the **subject list**, not just the stream.

## 6.2 Where Classes 11–12 are taught

| Setting | Student's likely wording | Note |
|---------|--------------------------|------|
| A school running Classes 1–12 or 11–12 | "I did 12th at my school" | Most common |

## 9.3 How vocational sits in the hierarchy — the NCrF unification

**This is India's most significant recent structural change and the AI should understand it.** ✅ (NCrF report, read directly)

The NCrF assigns **a single set of credit levels (1–8) across all three streams**:

| Stream | NCrF level range |
|--------|-----------------:|
| **School education** | up to Level 4 (Grade 5 = L1, Grade 8 = L2, Grade 10 = L3, Grade 12 = L4) |
| **Higher education** | **Level 4.5 to Level 8** (UG: 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0; PG: 6.0, 6.5, 7.0; PhD: 8) |
| **Vocational education, training and skilling** | **Level 1 to Level 8** (Level 1 = lowest competence/complexity, Level 8 = highest) |

Stated NCrF principles ✅ (quoted/paraphrased from the report):
- *"Creditization of all types of learnings; Assignments of Credit levels for all learnings"*
- *"Assignment of one single Credit level i.e. NCrF Credits Levels applicable across all qualification frameworks; no other separate Credit levels to be assigned by any"* other framework
- *"The total Notional Learning Hours for assignment of credits to be uniform across"* streams
- *"Provision for Recognition of Prior Learning, and NCrF credit levels and credit"* assignment for RPL

> **Counselling significance:** the NCrF is India's mechanism for making vocational and academic learning **commensurable on one scale**. In principle, a vocational qualification at NCrF Level 4 is at the same credit level as Class 12. **In practice**, whether a specific university will admit a specific vocational-qualification holder is still an **institutional decision**. ⚠ [PERIODIC]

## 10.3 The 3-year vs 4-year bachelor's question

**Both are currently valid Indian bachelor's degrees:**

| Degree | Duration | Credits | NHEQF level | Master's entry |
|--------|---------:|--------:|------------:|----------------|
| Bachelor's | 3 years | 120 | 5.5 | **2-year** master's |
| Bachelor's (Honours / Honours with Research) | 4 years | 160 | 6 | **1-year** master's |
| B.E. / B.Tech. | 4 years | 160 | 6 | 2-year M.E./M.Tech. |

✅ All from NHEQF Tables 2 and 11.

> ⚠⚠ **This is the highest-impact single fact for Indian students applying to master's programmes abroad.** India's own framework now recognises the 4-year Honours degree as a distinct, higher NHEQF level than the 3-year bachelor's, and grants it a **1-year master's** route domestically. Acceptance of **3-year Indian bachelor's degrees** for master's entry abroad **varies by destination and institution** — many UK, Australian and Canadian institutions accept them; US practice has historically varied and is institution-specific. ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) **Never state a blanket rule; always establish the duration and credits.**

## 10.4 Multiple entry / multiple exit (MEME)

The NHEQF explicitly builds in **multiple entry, multiple exit and re-entry** at undergraduate and master's levels ✅:

- Exit after **year 1** → **Undergraduate Certificate** (40 credits + 4-credit exit module)
- Exit after **year 2** → **Undergraduate Diploma** (80 credits + 4-credit exit module)
- Exit after **year 3** → **Bachelor's Degree** (120 credits)
- Continue to **year 4** → **Bachelor's (Honours / Honours with Research)** (160 credits)
- Exit after **year 1 of a 2-year master's** → **Post-Graduate Diploma** (40 credits)

**Mechanism:** the **Academic Bank of Credits (ABC)** — students open an "Academic Bank Account" to transfer and consolidate credits earned across eligible HEIs nationally. ✅

**Credit validity:** *"the validity of credits earned and kept in the Academic Credit Account will be to a maximum period of seven years or as specified by the ABC"*. After seven years, **re-entry requires validation of prior learning**. ✅

**Lateral entry:** *"Lateral entry into the programme of study at a particular NHEQF level will be based on the validation of prior learning outcomes, including those achieved outside of formal learning or through learning and training in the workplace or in the community, through continuing professional development activities, or through independent/self-directed/self-managed learning activities."* ✅

> ⚠ **Counselling implication:** an Indian applicant may hold an **Undergraduate Certificate** or **Undergraduate Diploma** that is a *formal NEP exit award*, not a "failed degree". These are genuine NHEQF-level qualifications (Level 4.5 and 5, 40 and 80 credits). The AI must recognise them as such — and must also recognise that they are **not bachelor's degrees**.

## 11.1 The NCrF — India's unifying credit framework ✅

Read directly from *National Credit Framework (NCrF): The Report of the High-Level Committee*.

**Credit-level assignment** — *"based on the cumulative numbers of years of learning with assessment"*:

| Stage | NCrF credit level |
|-------|------------------:|
| Completion of **Grade 5** | **Level 1** |
| Completion of **Grade 8** | **Level 2** |
| Completion of **Grade 10** | **Level 3** |
| Completion of **Grade 12** | **Level 4** |
| Higher education | **Level 4.5 → 8** (UG: 4.5, 5.0, 5.5, 6.0; PG: 6.0, 6.5, 7.0; PhD: 8) |
| Vocational education, training and skilling | **Level 1 → 8** |

**The credit arithmetic — memorise this, it is unusually clean:** ✅

| Rule | Value |
|------|------:|
| Notional learning hours per year (school, higher and vocational education alike) | **1,200 hours** |
| Credits awarded per year | **40 credits** |
| **Therefore: 1 credit** | **30 notional learning hours** |
| Credits per semester | **20 credits** (600 notional hours) |
| Minimum credits earnable in a year | **40** |
| Exit module (for multiple-exit awards) | **+4 credits** — e.g. exiting after year 1 of UG gives **44 total credits** |
| Additional learning beyond 1,200 hours | Earns **additional credits** |

Exclusion: the 1,200-hour rule applies *"except for pre-school up to grade 5th"*. ✅

> ✅ **This is one of the most useful facts in this whole document.** India has a **published, national, arithmetic definition of a credit** (1 credit = 30 notional learning hours; 40 credits = 1 year). Very few of the eight countries in this research have anything this explicit. It gives the AI a defensible basis for describing an Indian credit load — **without inventing a conversion to any other country's credit system**.

**Level descriptors:** the NCrF does not define its own descriptors; it delegates them — *"The level descriptors prescribed for a NCrF credit levels will be defined for every qualification framework i.e. NHEQF, NSQF and NCF."* ✅

**NHEQF descriptor elements** (per NCrF) ✅: knowledge and understanding; skills required to perform and accomplish tasks; application of knowledge and skills; generic learning outcomes; constitutional/humanistic/ethical/moral values and employment-ready and entrepreneurship skills; **credit requirements**; **entry requirements** for deciding the NCrF level.

## 11.2 The NHEQF — higher education ✅

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Official name | **National Higher Education Qualifications Framework (NHEQF)** |
| Authority | **UGC**, Government of India, Ministry of Education, Department of Higher Education ✅ |
| Levels | **4.5, 5, 5.5, 6, 6.5, 7, 8** — *"NHEQF level 4.5 represents learning outcomes appropriate to the first year (first two semesters) of the undergraduate programme of study, while Level 8 represents learning outcomes appropriate to the doctoral-level programme of study"* ✅ |
| Basis | **Outcome-based** — *"Each NHEQF level is structured based on the defined learning outcomes which lead to the expected graduate attributes/profile"* ✅ |

## 11.3 Framework maturity

⚠ **These frameworks are recent and implementation is incomplete.** The NHEQF and NCrF are authoritative statements of India's **intended** architecture, and are the correct reference for what an Indian qualification *is designed to be*. But:
- Many Indian universities were still awarding **3-year bachelor's degrees under pre-NEP regulations** as of this research.
- **State adoption of the 4-year UG programme is uneven.** ⚠ [VOLATILE]
- A **systematic literature review of NEP 2020 implementation (Frontiers in Education, 2026)** found that **41 of 50 reviewed studies identified significant implementation barriers** — infrastructural gaps, faculty readiness, and regional disparities — while 39/50 reported positive Choice-Based Credit System adoption. ✅ (see §27)

> **AI rule:** cite the NHEQF/NCrF as *what the qualification is defined as*, and simultaneously **ask the student what their actual transcript says** (duration, credits, year). Framework and reality do not yet coincide everywhere in India.

## 12.1 Higher education — the UGC/NHEQF 10-point letter scale ✅

Read directly from **NHEQF Table 12**:

| Letter grade | Grade point |
|--------------|------------:|
| **O** (Outstanding) | **10** |
| **A+** (Excellent) | **9** |
| **A** (Very good) | **8** |
| **B+** (Good) | **7** |
| **B** (Above average) | **6** |
| **C** (Average) | **5** |
| **P** (Pass) | **4** |
| **F** (Fail) | **0** |
| **Ab** (Absent) | **0** |

*(⚠ The source PDF's table extracts with a one-row column offset; the alignment above is the standard UGC CBCS 10-point scale and is consistent with the extracted grade-point sequence 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 0, 0. Confirm against the published NHEQF PDF before treating as exact.)*

**Critical delegation clause** ✅ — quoted from the NHEQF:

> *"For non-credit courses 'Satisfactory' or 'Unsatisfactory' will be indicated instead of the letter grade and this will not be counted for the computation of SGPA/CGPA. **The universities or the autonomous colleges can decide on the grade or percentage of marks required to pass in a course and the CGPA required to qualify for a Certificate/Diploma/Degree** taking into consideration the recommendations of the relevant standard setting body."*

> ✅ **This is the authoritative basis for the rule the AI must apply:** **India has a nationally recommended 10-point grade scale, but pass marks and degree-classification CGPA thresholds are set by the individual university or autonomous college.** The AI can state the 10-point scale as national practice; it must **not** state a national pass mark or classification threshold for higher education.

**CGPA definition** ✅ (NHEQF): *"The CGPA is the ratio of total credit points secured by a student in various courses"* — i.e. credit-weighted, computed over all semesters. **SGPA** is the semester equivalent.

## 12.2 Higher education — percentage and class/division

Many Indian universities report — or additionally report — **percentage marks with class/division bands**:

| Band | Conventional meaning |
|------|---------------------|
| **First Class with Distinction** | Highest band |
| **First Class / First Division** | — |
| **Second Class / Second Division** | — |
| **Third Class / Third Division / Pass Class** | — |

> ⚠ **The percentage boundaries for these bands are institution-specific.** A 7.5 CGPA maps to different percentage bands at different universities, and **many universities issue their own conversion certificates**. ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) **Never apply one CGPA→percentage formula nationally.**

❌ *Any national CGPA→percentage conversion for Indian higher education — does not exist. Treat as non-existent.*

**What the AI should do instead:** use (a) the **issuing university's own conversion certificate**, or (b) the **destination institution's published method**. Foreign evaluators either use the issuing university's conversion or their own internal methodology (e.g. WES iGPA), and **conversions differ between evaluators**. ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base)

## 12.3 CBSE Class 12 — positional (relative) grading ⚠

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Scale | **9-point grading scale**, A1 (highest) down through D2, with **E / E1 / E2** for fail ⚠ |
| **Grading method** | **Positional / relative** — grades are assigned by the student's **rank among all candidates who passed that subject nationally**, dividing passers into **eight equal segments (octiles)**: A1 (top eighth) down to D2 (lowest eighth of passers) ⚠ |
| Marks also reported | **Yes** — the marksheet shows subject marks *and* the grade ⚠ |
| Pass criteria | **33% in each subject**; for subjects with practical components, **33% separately in theory and in practical**, plus **33% aggregate** ⚠ |
| CGPA | Averaged grade points across **five main subjects** ⚠ |
| CGPA → percentage | **CGPA × 9.5** ⚠ |

### ⚠⚠ A documented source conflict on CBSE grading

| Claim | Statement |
|-------|-----------|
| **Claim A (positional)** | Grades are assigned by **national rank in octiles** among passers — A1 = top eighth |
| **Claim B (absolute)** | **A1 = 91–100 marks**, A2 = 81–90, etc. — fixed mark bands |

**Both claims appear in current secondary sources.** The most likely reconciliation is that **CBSE Class 12 uses positional/relative grading**, while the **absolute 91–100 = A1 bands belong to CBSE Class 10** (and to the CGPA era). But this was **not resolved from a CBSE primary source**, and the two produce very different readings of the same letter grade.

❌ *Authoritative CBSE grading rules for Class 10 and Class 12 separately — requires reading CBSE's official examination bye-laws / result-processing documents. **This is a priority gap.***

> ⚠ **AI rule:** if a CBSE Class 12 grade is **positional**, then **"A1" means "top eighth of the national cohort in that subject", not "91%+"**. The AI must not convert a CBSE Class 12 letter grade into a percentage. **Use the marks, which CBSE also reports.**

## 12.4 CBSE Class 10 — three eras ⚠

| Era | Reporting form |
|-----|---------------|
| Before ~2011 | **Percentage marks** |
| **~2011–2017** | **CGPA on a 10-point scale**, with a published **× 9.5** conversion to percentage ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) |
| ~2017 onward | **Return to marks** (plus grades) ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) |

> ⚠ **AI rule:** an Indian applicant with a **CBSE Class 10 CGPA** studied in roughly **2011–2017**. The **× 9.5** conversion is one of the **very few officially published conversions in Indian education** — it was CBSE's own. Use it **only** for CBSE Class 10 CGPA of that era, and for nothing else.

## 12.5 CISCE — ICSE and ISC ⚠

| Item | ICSE (Class 10) | ISC (Class 12) |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| Grade scale | Grade points **1 to 9**, where **1 is the highest** ⚠ | Grades **1 to 9**, where **1–7 = pass, 8–9 = fail** ⚠ |
| Fail grades | **8 and 9** ⚠ | **8 and 9** ⚠ |
| Pass criteria | Reported as **33% in theory and internal assessment**; a separate source states **35 out of 100 in every subject** ⚠ **conflict** | Reported as **40% in each subject** and **35% aggregate** ⚠ |
| 2026 pass rate | **99.18%** ⚠ (news source) | **99.14%** ⚠ (news source) |

⚠ **Note the inverted grade direction:** at CISCE, **1 is the best grade and 9 is the worst**. This is the opposite direction to CBSE (A1 best) and to most other systems the AI will see. **Misreading a CISCE "1" as poor performance would be a serious error.**

⚠ **Note the different pass thresholds:** ICSE and ISC appear to use **different** pass marks (33/35% vs 40%), and sources conflict on ICSE. ❌ *Authoritative CISCE pass criteria and grading tables — requires reading CISCE's official regulations at cisce.org. **Priority gap.***

## 12.6 State boards ⚠

| Item | Status |
|------|--------|
| Dominant reporting form | **Percentage marks per subject** ⚠ |
| Aggregate practice | Universities commonly use **"best of four"** or **"best of five"** subject averages for admission ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base) |
| Pass marks | Vary by board (commonly around 33–35%) ⚠ |
| Grading scales | Vary by board; some boards issue grades alongside marks ⚠ |
| Marking severity | **Materially different across boards** — a 90% from one board is not the same achievement as 90% from another ⚠ |

❌ **This is the single largest grading gap for India: a board-by-board table of grading scales, pass marks and mark distributions.** Requires per-board research.

## 12.7 Summary — grading systems the AI may encounter from one Indian student

A single Indian applicant can plausibly present **all** of the following:

| Document | Likely grading form |
|----------|--------------------|
| Class 10 marksheet | Percentage marks, or CGPA (CBSE 2011–17), or grades 1–9 (CISCE), or board-specific grades |
| Class 12 marksheet | Percentage marks **and** a 9-point positional grade (CBSE), or grades 1–9 (CISCE), or percentage only (most state boards) |
| Bachelor's transcript | 10-point CGPA (post-CBCS), **or** percentage with First/Second/Third Class, **or** both |

| Conversion | Status |

# 15. Credit Systems

**India is unusual among the eight countries in having a published national credit arithmetic. Use it.**

| Context | Unit | Definition | Verification |
|---------|------|-----------|--------------|
| **All streams (NCrF)** | credit | **1 credit = 30 notional learning hours**; **40 credits = 1 year (1,200 hours)**; **20 credits = 1 semester (600 hours)** | ✅ NCrF §3.2.4, §3.2.7 |
| **Higher education (NHEQF)** | credit | Same; qualification credit minima: UG Certificate 40, UG Diploma 80, Bachelor's 120, Bachelor's Honours / B.E./B.Tech. 160, PG Diploma 40, Master's 80 (after 3-yr bachelor's) or 40 (after 4-yr Honours) | ✅ NHEQF Table 11 |
| **Exit modules** | credit | **+4 credits** skills-enhancement module required for the UG Certificate and UG Diploma exit awards | ✅ NHEQF Table 1 |
| **Vocational (NSQF)** | credit | Credits earned for short-term and long-term skilling, assigned NCrF levels | ✅ NCrF |
| **Credit transfer** | — | Via the **Academic Bank of Credits (ABC)** — a national credit bank with **individual student accounts** | ✅ NHEQF §4.5 |
| **Credit validity** | — | **Maximum 7 years** in the Academic Credit Account; after that, re-entry requires **validation of prior learning** | ✅ NHEQF §4.5 |
| **Recognition of prior learning** | — | Provided for in the NCrF; lateral entry based on validation of prior learning including workplace, community, CPD and self-directed learning | ✅ NHEQF §4.5, NCrF |
| **Pre-NEP credits (CBCS)** | credit | Older Indian transcripts use **Choice-Based Credit System (CBCS)** credits, which pre-date the NCrF's uniform 30-hour definition ⚠ | ⚠ |
| **Legacy annual-system degrees** | *(none)* | Older Indian degrees have **no credits at all** — subjects and marks only ⚠ | ⚠ |

> ⚠ **Do not assume an Indian credit equals a credit anywhere else.** India defines a credit as **30 notional learning hours** and a year as **40 credits**. Other systems in this research define credits differently (e.g. ECTS 25–30 hours with 60/year; US semester credits ~15 contact hours with ~30/year). **These are different currencies. The AI must not convert between them; it should state each system's own definition.**

> ⚠ **Also do not assume an Indian transcript has credits.** A pre-CBCS, annual-system Indian degree lists **subjects and marks**, not credits. Asking a student for their "total credits" may produce confusion or a fabricated number.

# 23. International Recognition Context (descriptive only)

**What exactly is an "Indian Class 12" qualification?**

- It is a **senior secondary school-leaving certificate** issued by **one of India's many examination boards** — CBSE (AISSCE), CISCE (ISC), NIOS, or a state board (HSC / Intermediate / II PUC / etc.). **The board matters**; there is no single "Indian Class 12". ✅
- It follows **two years of study (Classes 11–12)** after a **Class 10 board examination**, itself following **10 years of schooling** — a total of **12 years**. ✅
- Under India's own **NCrF**, Class 12 completion is **credit Level 4**, and higher education begins at **Level 4.5**. ✅
- It is **India's university-entrance qualification**, though for a growing set of universities the **CUET** score now does the selecting while Class 12 provides eligibility. ⚠
- Results are usually reported as **percentage marks per subject**; CBSE additionally reports a **9-point positional grade**; CISCE reports **grades 1–9 with 1 as best**. ⚠
- Students choose a **stream** (Science PCM / Science PCB / Commerce / Humanities / Vocational), and the **subject combination determines eligibility** for specific higher-education programmes. ⚠
- **Common aliases:** "Class 12", "12th", "+2", "10+2", "senior secondary", "higher secondary", "HSC", "Intermediate", "II PUC", "AISSCE", "ISC".

**What exactly is an "Indian Class 10" qualification?**
- The **Class 10 board examination** — AISSE (CBSE), ICSE (CISCE), SSC, SSLC, High School, Madhyamik, or a state equivalent. **NCrF Level 3.** It is a mid-secondary checkpoint and the gateway to Classes 11–12 or to the polytechnic/ITI streams. **It is not a school-leaving qualification for higher education.** ✅

**What exactly is an "Indian bachelor's degree"?**
- **Either** a **3-year, 120-credit** bachelor's degree at **NHEQF level 5.5**, **or** a **4-year, 160-credit** Bachelor's (Honours / Honours with Research) at **NHEQF level 6** — **both are currently valid and both are being awarded.** Professional degrees (B.E./B.Tech. 4 yr/160 cr, B.Arch. 5 yr, B.Pharm. 4 yr, MBBS ~5.5 yr) sit alongside. Taught at one of **45,473 colleges** but **awarded by one of ~1,168 universities**. ✅

**What exactly is an "Indian Diploma"?**
- ⚠ **Ambiguous, and a frequent source of error.** It may be:
  - a **3-year polytechnic Diploma in Engineering after Class 10** (a vocational/technical award taken *instead of* Classes 11–12);
  - an **NEP Undergraduate Diploma** (2 years of a bachelor's programme, 80 credits, NHEQF level 5);
  - a **Post-Graduate Diploma** (1 year after a bachelor's, 40 credits, NHEQF level 6);
  - a **PGDM** from a standalone management institute.
- **The AI must always ask: after Class 10, after Class 12, or after a bachelor's?** ✅

*Cross-country equivalency is deliberately out of scope for this document.*

# 26. India-Specific Complexity Summary

The seven things that most often go wrong when interpreting an Indian student's education:

1. **"Which board?" is the first question.** ~30+ state boards plus CBSE, CISCE and NIOS, each with its own grading, pass criteria and certificate name. (§8)
2. **"(Hons)" is ambiguous** — 3-year specialised honours (pre-NEP) vs. 4-year NEP Honours (160 credits). Ask the duration. (§2.2, §10.3)
3. **3-year vs 4-year bachelor's** — both are currently valid, at different NHEQF levels, with different domestic master's routes. (§10.3)
4. **"Diploma" has four meanings** — polytechnic (post-Class-10), NEP UG Diploma, PG Diploma, PGDM. (§23)
5. **CISCE grades run 1 (best) to 9 (worst)** — the opposite direction to almost everything else. (§12.5)
6. **CBSE Class 12 grading is positional**, so a letter grade means a rank position, not a mark band. Use the marks. (§12.3)
7. **India is mid-reform.** NEP 2020 / NHEQF / NCrF / NCF-SE define the intended system; actual transcripts often reflect the old one. Cite the framework, then read the transcript. (§11.3)

Plus: **"junior college" and "PU college" are Classes 11–12**, not higher education (§6.2); **45,473 colleges award degrees on behalf of ~1,168 universities** (§10.1); and **CGPA→percentage has no national formula** — ask for the university's conversion certificate (§13, §14.2).
