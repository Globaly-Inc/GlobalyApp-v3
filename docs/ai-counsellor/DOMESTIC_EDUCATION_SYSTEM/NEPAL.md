## 1.2 Governance: what is national vs. sub-national

Nepal became a **federal republic** under the **Constitution of Nepal (2015)**. Education authority is split three ways: [STABLE]

| Tier | Units | Education responsibility |
|------|-------|--------------------------|
| Federal | Government of Nepal / MoEST | National policy, curriculum framework (CDC), national examinations (NEB), higher education (via UGC), TVET framework (CTEVT/NSTB), qualification framework |
| Provincial | **7 provinces** (Koshi, Madhesh, Bagmati, Gandaki, Lumbini, Karnali, Sudurpashchim) | Provincial education policy, Provincial Education Training Centres (PETC), some secondary-level functions |
| Local | **753 local levels** (municipalities / rural municipalities) | Constitutionally assigned **basic and secondary education** management; school-level administration |

✅ The seven provinces are the reporting units in Flash I Report 2081; the 753 local levels are named as the full IEMIS coverage since BS 2074 (2017/18).

**What is nationally standardised (the AI can rely on this country-wide):**
- The **grade structure** (Grades 1–12) and the **level definitions** (Basic = 1–8, Secondary = 9–12). ✅
- The **national curriculum** issued by CDC. ✅
- The **two national school examinations**: SEE (Grade 10) and NEB Grade 12. ✅
- The **letter-grading system** for Grades 1–12 under the *Letter Grading Directive, 2078*. ⚠ (directive named by multiple sources; read the directive for boundaries)
- The **National Qualifications Framework** (8 levels, approved 3 May 2020). ✅

**What varies:**
- **Higher-education grading** — set per university, not nationally. ✅ (See §12.4.)
- **Bachelor's degree length** — 3 or 4 years depending on university and programme. ⚠
- **Medium of instruction** — Nepali vs. English, varying by school and by community/institutional status. ⚠
- **School quality and resourcing** — the community/institutional (public/private) split is large and geographically uneven. ✅ (see §17)

> Nepal is **far less board-fragmented than India or Bangladesh**: there is essentially **one school board (NEB)** plus the **CTEVT** vocational stream, not dozens of state boards. This is a key structural difference the AI should not blur.

**The 2016/2017 structural reform (critical for reading transcripts):**
The Education Regulations amendment **abolished the older four-tier legal structure** — primary (1–5), lower secondary (6–8), secondary (9–10), higher secondary (11–12) — and replaced it with the **two-tier** structure: Basic (1–8) and Secondary (9–12). ⚠ [STABLE]

**Consequence for the AI:** a Nepali student's transcript vocabulary depends on *when* they studied.

| Era | Grade 10 exam name | Grade 12 authority | Grade 12 name on certificate |
|-----|--------------------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| Pre-2016 | **SLC** (School Leaving Certificate) — historically called the "Iron Gate" | **HSEB** (Higher Secondary Education Board) | Higher Secondary Education Certificate / "Intermediate" / "+2" |
| 2016/2017 onward | **SEE** (Secondary Education Examination) | **NEB** (National Examinations Board) | NEB Grade 12 / Class 12 / "+2" |

✅ The NEB replaced the HSEB in 2016 (same qualification lineage). Note the terminology trap: **"SLC" now means something different** — see §1.4.

## 1.4 A naming trap the AI must handle

The NQF document (CTEVT/NSTB, Sept 2019) reuses the abbreviation **SLC** for the *Grade 12* general-education qualification:

> "After the implementation of new education Act 2016, the final examination that will take place in grade 12 is **School Leaving Certificate Exam**, an individual who wants to study bachelor need to go complete and get a pass in School Leaving Certificate Exam. … Eligibility to get SLC certificate is a two-year full-time study in grade 11 and 12." ✅ (CTEVT/NSTB NQF Short Explanation, Sept 2019)

So:
- **"SLC" pre-2016** = the **Grade 10** school-leaving exam.
- **"SLC" in NQF/post-2016 policy language** = the **Grade 12** school-leaving exam.
- In everyday Nepali usage and on NEB documents, the Grade 12 award is still normally called **"Grade 12" / "+2" / "Class 12"**, and Grade 10 is **"SEE"**.

⚠ **AI rule:** never resolve a bare "SLC" without asking the student *which grade* and *which year*. Both readings are legitimate.

# 2. Education Levels — Actual Structure

```text
ECED / Pre-primary  (≥1 year, age ~4–5, not compulsory)
        ↓
BASIC EDUCATION  — Grades 1–8, ages ~5–12, compulsory & free
        ├── (legacy sub-labels: "primary" 1–5, "lower secondary" 6–8 — abolished in law)
        ↓
SECONDARY EDUCATION — Grades 9–12, ages ~13–16, free (not compulsory)
        ├── Grades 9–10  → SEE (Secondary Education Examination), end of Grade 10
        └── Grades 11–12 → NEB Grade 12 Examination ("+2")
                ↓                                    ↘
        HIGHER EDUCATION                      TVET STREAM (CTEVT)
        ├── Bachelor (3 or 4 yr)              ├── Short vocational training
        ├── Master (2 yr)                     ├── Pre-Diploma (formerly TSLC), 18 months, after SEE
        ├── MPhil (where offered)             └── Diploma, 3 yr, after SEE
        └── PhD                                       ↓
                                              (Diploma → Bachelor entry ⚠ §22)
```

## 2.2 Legacy terminology map (essential for older applicants)

| Legacy term | Legacy meaning | Current equivalent |
|-------------|----------------|--------------------|
| **SLC** (pre-2016) | Grade 10 school-leaving exam ("Iron Gate") | **SEE** (Grade 10) |
| **HSEB** | Higher Secondary Education Board | **NEB** |

## 5.1 What the SEE is and is not

- The SEE is **Nepal's Grade 10 examination**. It replaced the **SLC** after the 2016 Education Act. ✅
- The historic SLC was famously called the **"Iron Gate"** because of high failure rates; the post-2016 SEE moved to letter grading, and **"failing" was replaced by non-graded (NG)** results in most subjects. ⚠ (structural change well-attested; read the *Letter Grading Directive 2078* for the operative rules)
- **The SEE alone does not qualify anyone for university, anywhere.** It is a mid-secondary checkpoint. The AI must never treat an SEE result as a school-leaving credential for higher education. ✅

❌ *Definitive current SEE subject list and per-subject theory/practical weightings — requires reading NEB's published SEE examination specification.*

## 6.1 Where "+2" is actually taught (a real source of student confusion)

Grades 11–12 in Nepal are delivered in three different institutional settings, and students describe them differently:

| Setting | Student's likely wording | Notes |
|---------|--------------------------|-------|
| A **secondary school** running Grades 1–12 or 9–12 | "I did +2 at my school" | 5,049 schools run Grades 1–12 ✅ |
| A **"+2 college"** / higher-secondary college (often private) | "I went to XYZ College for +2" | **"College" here means a Grade 11–12 school, not higher education.** Critical terminology trap. |
| A **university-affiliated campus** offering +2 | "I did +2 at a TU campus" | Award is still an NEB Grade 12, not a university credential |

> ⚠ **AI rule:** when a Nepali student says **"college"**, it very often means the institution where they did **Grades 11–12**, *not* a higher-education institution. Always resolve "college" by asking what qualification was awarded.

## 6.2 Streams / subject structure

Under the current curriculum, students **choose a major subject on enrolling in Grade 11**, rather than enrolling into a named faculty as under the old HSEB system:

> "After completing Grade 10, students choose their major subject when enrolling in Grade 11, rather than selecting from different faculties in the past. Currently, there are more than 12 subjects to choose from." ✅ (Flash I Report 2081, §5.5)

**Legacy stream names still used constantly in conversation** (from the HSEB era and by colleges for marketing): **Science, Management, Humanities/Arts, Education, Law**. ⚠ These are not the current legal structure but remain the everyday vocabulary. A student saying "I did +2 Science" is describing a subject combination, not a formally named faculty.

**Subject structure (compulsory + optional):**

Sources **disagree** on the compulsory-subject set, and this must be flagged rather than resolved silently:

| Source claim | Compulsory subjects stated |
|--------------|---------------------------|
| Claim A | Nepali, English, **Social Studies** (Grade 11) and **Jiwan Upayogi / Life-skills** (Grade 12) |
| Claim B | Nepali, English, **Nepal Study**, and a **skill-based subject** — 4 compulsory + 3 optional |

⚠ Both come from secondary Nepali education sites. **Do not state a compulsory-subject list as fact.** The authoritative source is the **CDC Grade 11–12 curriculum (2076)**. ❌ *Definitive compulsory/optional subject structure — requires reading the CDC curriculum document.*

**Credit hours:** secondary sources report Grade 11 totalling approximately **27 credit hours** across compulsory plus three optional subjects, with a typical subject carrying **4 credit hours / 128 annual working hours**. ⚠ Unverified against CDC. A "credit hour" at Grade 11–12 in Nepal is a **curriculum workload unit, not a transferable higher-education credit** — see §15.

**Assessment split:** commonly **75 marks theory + 25 marks internal assessment** per 100-mark subject, with practical-heavy subjects (e.g. Computer Science) reported at **50 theory + 50 practical**. ⚠ secondary sources; confirm at NEB.

## 9.3 The NVQF layer (competency-based, separate from CTEVT programme awards)

Per the CTEVT/NSTB *National Qualifications Framework of Nepal: Short Explanation* (Sept 2019) ✅:

| TVET qualification title | Definition as stated |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| **National Technician Certificate (NTC)** | Basic-education completers with occupational knowledge/skill via apprenticeship or prescribed training; **or** ≥5 years' relevant work experience with required literacy; **or** 2 years' experience after 1-year training; **or** 1 year's experience after a skill-level-2 certificate; **or** via **RPL assessment** |

| **Masters (Technical)** | 2-year approved programme after Bachelor |
| **PhD** | 3-year approved programme, research-based |

**Permeability rules stated in the NQF document** ✅ — these are the concrete progression mechanisms:

- **NVQ Level 3** holder → eligible to enrol in the **National Technician Certificate Programme (NTCP)**, subject to NTCP entry criteria.
- **NVQ Level 4** holder → eligible to enrol in the **National Diploma Programme (NDP)**.
- **NVQ Level 4 and NTC holders** → eligible to enrol in the **School Leaving Certificate Programme (SLCP)** — i.e. **TVET → general-education Grade 12**. Described in the document as *"a new arrangement provisioned for the first time in Nepal."*
- **NVQ Level 5 and National Diploma holders** → eligible to enrol in a **Technical Bachelor's Degree** and in a general **Bachelor's Degree Programme (BDP)**, subject to entry criteria.
- **NVQ Level 6 ≡ bachelor's degree** in technical/vocational education; **NVQ Level 7 ≡ master's degree**; **NVQ Level 8 ≡ PhD by research** — as stated in the framework document.

> ⚠ **Critical caveat:** the NQF document is a **framework explanation authored in Sept 2019** by CTEVT/NSTB with Swisscontact support, describing the framework *submitted for approval*; the NQF was **approved by the Government of Nepal on 3 May 2020** ✅. Whether every stated permeability rule is **operationally implemented today** (e.g. whether universities actually admit ND holders to Bachelor programmes as of 2026) is a separate question. The AI should present these as **framework provisions**, not as guaranteed admission outcomes. [PERIODIC]

## 11.3 Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)

✅ Directly from the NQF document:

- RPL is the **"process of recognition of already gained skills of the individuals (with or without portfolios) to enable them to acquire NVQ after going through assessment system."**
- The framework provisions that candidates **from both streams** (General Education and TVET) with non-formal/informal learning **can earn an NVQ level**, via **Qualification Assessment (QA)** plus a **soft-skills assessment ("QA+")**.
- The NVQ level "will be granted by **National Vocational Qualifications Authority**" after assessment against competency standards.

**Counselling relevance:** a Nepali applicant with **substantial work experience but thin formal schooling** has a domestic route to a recognised qualification level. Whether any given destination country honours an RPL-derived NVQ level is a **separate, out-of-scope question**.

## 12.2 Pass criteria — a documented source conflict

| Source claim | Stated requirement |
|--------------|--------------------|
| Claim A | Minimum **Grade D (1.6 GPA, 35%)** in every individual subject; **no overall aggregate minimum** — each subject passed individually |

| Claim C (SEE) | Each SEE student must obtain **35 in theory** out of 100 full marks to be qualified for higher studies |

**Research update (2026-08-21):** a **fourth** variant emerged, and it contradicts Claim A directly:

| Source claim | Stated requirement |
|--------------|--------------------|
| **Claim E (new)** | *"You must score at least **Grade D which is 35 percent or GPA 1.6 in every individual subject**, **AND have an overall GPA of 2.0 or 50 percent to pass**, meaning you must both pass every individual subject and have a decent overall average."* ⚠ |

⚠ **The conflict is now wider, not narrower:**
- Claim B references a **"D+" grade that does not appear in the grade table** (which runs …C, D, NG).
- Claims C and D differ on whether the 35% threshold applies to **theory alone** or **theory + practical combined**.
- **Claim A says there is NO aggregate minimum; Claim E says there IS one (overall GPA 2.0).** These are directly contradictory and both come from Nepali education sources.

**Still not resolved.** ❌ *Authoritative pass criteria for SEE and NEB Grade 12 — requires the Letter Grading Directive 2078 text and NEB's current examination regulations. **This remains a blocker** and the additional variant makes it more urgent, not less.*

**What is safe for the AI to say:** Nepal's school grading requires a **per-subject minimum of around 35% (grade D / GPA 1.6)**; results below threshold are reported as **NG (Non-Graded)** rather than "fail"; and **whether an additional overall-GPA minimum applies is disputed among sources**. ### ✅ What the research update DID establish about the directive

**The Letter Grading Directive 2078's contents are now documented** ⚠ (from Nepali education sources describing the directive itself, not merely quoting numbers from it):

- Issued by **MoEST**, covering **Classes 1 to 12**
- Developed *"in line with the **National Curriculum Framework for School Education, 2076**"*
- Stated aim: to make student evaluation *"more relevant, transparent, objective, and reliable"*
- **It defines** *"key terms such as **credit hour, letter grading, and grade point**"*
- **It specifies** *"how marks obtained in **internal, practical, and theoretical assessments** are converted into letter grades"*
- **It covers** *"grade categories, achievement levels, **grade improvement examinations**, certification requirements, and the roles of concerned bodies including the Ministry, Curriculum Development Center, National Examination Board, Education Development and Coordination Units, and schools"*

> ✅ **This confirms the directive is the correct authority** for: the grade boundaries, the internal/practical/theory conversion, credit hours, and grade-improvement rules — i.e. everything currently marked ⚠ in this section. It is the single highest-value acquisition remaining for Nepal.

⚠ Reported by secondary sources (a `portal.tu.edu.np` grading-policy PDF exists but returned **HTTP 404** during this research). **Note this scale is US-style (4.0/3.7/3.3) and therefore structurally different from the NEB school scale (4.0/3.6/3.2).** A Nepali student can legitimately hold **two 4.0-scale GPAs computed on two different step patterns**. The AI must not mix them.

**TU semester system — reported rules** ⚠:
- Semester bylaws revised, applicable from academic year **2074/75 (2017 intake)** onward, across semester programmes (BBS, BSc CSIT, BHM, BBA, BBM, BIT, MBS, BCA, etc.).
- End-of-semester external exam pass mark reported as **50% (grade point 2.7)**.
- A **minimum grade B or CGPA 3.0 (~60%)** reported as required to pass the semester examination.
- GPA formula: **GPA = Σ(grade points × credit hours) / Σ(credit hours)** — standard credit-weighted.

⚠ These rules are reported with some internal tension (a 2.7 pass per paper vs. a 3.0 CGPA requirement). ❌ *Authoritative TU grading and examination bylaws — requires the current TU Examination Control Division / Deans' Office document. The `cded.tu.edu.np` page states only that grading follows "the guidelines of Deans' Office, Tribhuvan University."*

**Other universities:** KU, PokU, PU and others each publish their own scales. ❌ *Per-university grading tables for KU / PokU / PU / FWU / MWU / AFU — not researched.*

**Annual-system programmes:** TU historically used **percentage marks with Division bands** (Distinction / First / Second / Third) for annual-system programmes, and many still do. ⚠ A Nepali bachelor's transcript may therefore show **percentage + Division** rather than GPA.

> **AI rule for Nepali higher-education grades:** treat university grading as **institution-specific**; **read the scale printed on the transcript itself**. Never apply the NEB school scale to a university transcript, and never assume GPA rather than percentage.

# 23. International Recognition Context (descriptive only)

**What exactly is a "Nepal Grade 12" qualification?**

- It is the **NEB Grade 12 certificate**, awarded by Nepal's **National Examinations Board**, on completion of **two years of full-time study in Grades 11 and 12** following the **SEE** (Grade 10). ✅
- It is the terminal qualification of **Nepal's secondary education level (Grades 9–12)**, mapped to **ISCED 3 (upper secondary)** in Nepal's own NQF. ✅
- It is the **standard basis for entry to bachelor's degree study in Nepal**, and Nepal's NQF calls it the qualification "an individual who wants to study bachelor need to go complete and get a pass in." ✅
- It is reported in **letter grades per subject plus a GPA on a 4.0 scale** (0.4 step increments). ✅ / ⚠ on boundaries.
- It represents **12 years of schooling** in total (8 basic + 4 secondary). ✅
- Its holders are a **selected minority** of the age cohort — Grade 11–12 NER was **37.1%** in 2024/25. ✅
- **Common aliases on documents and in speech:** "+2", "Class 12", "Grade 12", "higher secondary", and — in NQF policy language — **"School Leaving Certificate"**. Pre-2016 the equivalent was the **HSEB Higher Secondary Education Certificate / "Intermediate"**. ✅

**What exactly is a "Nepal SEE"?**
- The **Grade 10** national examination, marking the end of the lower cycle of secondary education (ISCED 2 boundary), administered by NEB, successor to the **SLC**. It is **not** a school-leaving qualification for higher education. ✅

**What exactly is a "CTEVT Diploma"?**
- A **3-year technical qualification after SEE**, awarded by CTEVT through affiliated technical institutes/polytechnics — i.e. a **vocational award taken in place of Grades 11–12**, not after them. ⚠ Its treatment abroad differs from an academic +2. ✅ (existing Globaly knowledge base: no widely published comparability guidance for CTEVT awards)

*Cross-country equivalency is deliberately out of scope for this document.*

# 26. Nepal-Specific Complexity Summary

The five things that most often go wrong when interpreting a Nepali student's education:

1. **"SLC" is genuinely ambiguous** — Grade 10 pre-2016, Grade 12 in NQF language. Always resolve by year and grade. (§1.4)
2. **"College" usually means a Grade 11–12 institution**, not higher education. (§6.1)
3. **Two different 4.0 GPA scales** — NEB school (0.4 steps: 4.0/3.6/3.2…) vs. university (US-style: 4.0/3.7/3.3…). Mixing them misreads the student's record. (§12)
4. **Two grading eras** — post-2016 letter grades + GPA vs. pre-2016 percentage + Division. The student's study year decides which applies. (§12)
5. **Bachelor's degrees are 3 *or* 4 years**, and Nepal's own framework says "at least 4". Read the transcript; never assume. (§10.3)

Plus: the **affiliated-college model** means the *college name is not the awarding body* (§10.1), and some Nepali students hold **foreign qualifications (A-Level/IB) obtained in Nepal** (§6.3).
