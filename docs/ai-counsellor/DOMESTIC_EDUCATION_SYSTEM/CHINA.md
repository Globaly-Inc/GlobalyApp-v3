**Document purpose:** Reference knowledge so the GlobalyApp AI counsellor can interpret what a Chinese student's stated education *means inside China* before any cross-country comparison is attempted.
**Research date:** 2026-08-21
**Scope:** **Mainland China only.** Hong Kong SAR, Macau SAR and Taiwan operate **separate education systems** and are out of scope for this document.

> ## ⚠ THE FOUR THINGS THAT MOST OFTEN GO WRONG WITH CHINA
>
> 1. **China issues TWO separate documents for a completed degree** — a **graduation certificate (毕业证书)** and a **degree certificate (学位证书)**. They are verified separately, and *"it is possible to hold a graduation certificate without a degree certificate, and vice versa."* ✅ Asking only about "your degree" can miss half the record.
> 2. **"Zhuanke" (专科) is not a bachelor's degree.** It is a **2–3 year** short-cycle higher-education programme that yields a **graduation certificate but no bachelor's degree**. Students may still describe it in English as "college" or even "university". ⚠
> 3. **The gaokao is provincially administered.** Papers, subject models, scoring and admission cut-offs differ **by province** — a "gaokao score" is not nationally comparable. ✅
> 4. **Verification runs through one official portal: CHSI / CSSD.** Since **August 2022** the Ministry of Education has consolidated verification of **both** graduation and degree certificates under CSSD, with CHSI as *"the sole official portal"*. ✅ This is the authoritative check for any Chinese credential.

## 1.2 Governance: national vs provincial

**China is a unitary state with a nationally set structure but substantial provincial administration of examinations and admissions.**

**What is nationally standardised (safe for country-wide AI statements):**

| Item | Verification |
|------|--------------|
| The **6+3+3** twelve-year school structure | ✅ |
| **Nine-year compulsory education** (primary 1–6 + junior secondary 7–9), free | ✅ |
| The **three-tier degree system** (bachelor's / master's / doctoral) and the academic vs professional degree distinction, under the **2024 Degrees Law** | ✅ |
| The **zhuanke / benke** distinction in higher education | ✅ |
| The **dual-credential system** (graduation certificate + degree certificate) | ✅ |
| **CHSI/CSSD** as the single verification portal | ✅ |
| The **100-point marking scale with 60 as the pass mark** at *"almost all Chinese universities"* | ⚠ |
| The **equal-status principle** for vocational education under the 2022 Vocational Education Law | ✅ |

**What varies by province — and it matters a lot:**

| Item | Nature of variation | Verification |
|------|--------------------|--------------|
| **Gaokao subject model** | Two reformed models coexist: **"3+3"** (6 provincial-level regions incl. Beijing, Shanghai, Shandong) and **"3+1+2"** (23 provinces). By 2025, **29 provincial-level regions** had implemented a reformed gaokao. | ✅ |
| **Gaokao examination papers** | Some provinces use national papers, others set their own ⚠ | ⚠ |
| **Gaokao admission cut-offs and quotas** | Set **per province**, per institution — a score admitting a student in one province may not in another ⚠ | ⚠ |
| **Zhongkao** (senior-high entrance) | Administered **locally/provincially**, not nationally | ✅ |
| **Senior secondary graduation examination** | **Huikao (会考)** vs **Academic Proficiency Test (学业水平测试)** — differs by province and era | ✅ |
| **University grading and GPA conversion** | *"The Ministry of Education does not mandate a single national GPA scale"* — institutions differ ⚠ | ⚠ |
| **Where a student may sit the gaokao** | Tied to household registration (**hukou**) rather than simply where they live ⚠ | ⚠ |

> ⚠ **The provincial dimension of the gaokao is the most under-appreciated China fact.** Two students with the same raw gaokao score in different provinces face **different admission outcomes**, because cut-offs and institutional quotas are provincial. A gaokao score is therefore **only interpretable together with the province and year**. The AI must record both.

# 2. Education Levels — Actual Structure

```text
PRE-SCHOOL / KINDERGARTEN (幼儿园)  ages ~3–6, not compulsory
        ↓
PRIMARY SCHOOL (小学)              Grades 1–6, ages ~6–12   ┐
        ↓                                                    ├ NINE-YEAR COMPULSORY
JUNIOR SECONDARY (初中)            Grades 7–9, ages ~12–15  ┘  EDUCATION (free)
        ├── ZHONGKAO (中考) — junior-high graduation AND senior-high admission
        ↓
SENIOR SECONDARY  ages ~15–18, 3 years, NOT compulsory
        ├── GENERAL / ACADEMIC senior high school (普通高中)
        │     ├── Huikao (会考) or Academic Proficiency Test (学业水平测试) → graduation requirement
        │     └── Senior High School Graduation Diploma (普通高中毕业证书)
        │              ↓ GAOKAO (高考)
        └── VOCATIONAL senior secondary (中职: 中专 / 技校 / 职业高中)
                       ↓ (vocational entry routes)
        ↓                                          ↓
HIGHER EDUCATION                          VOCATIONAL HIGHER EDUCATION
├── ZHUANKE (专科) 2–3 yr                 ├── Higher vocational college (高职) = zhuanke level
│     → graduation certificate only       └── VOCATIONAL UNDERGRADUATE (职业本科)
│     → ZHUANSHENGBEN (专升本) → benke           = benke level, per the 2022 Law ✅
├── BENKE (本科) 4–5 yr
│     → graduation certificate + BACHELOR'S DEGREE certificate (学士)
├── MASTER'S (硕士) — academic or professional
└── DOCTORAL (博士) — academic or professional
```

## 2.2 The zhuanke / benke distinction — the most important China structural fact

| | **Zhuanke (专科)** | **Benke (本科)** |
|---|---|---|
| English rendering | "Junior college", "short-cycle", "associate", "college diploma" — **all inexact** | "Undergraduate", "bachelor's" |
| Duration | **2–3 years** ✅ | **4–5 years** ✅ |
| Orientation | *"more practically-oriented than bachelor's programmes"* ✅ | Academic |
| Institution | **Higher vocational colleges (高职)**, and zhuanke programmes at some universities ⚠ | Universities (大学) and colleges (学院) |
| Documents on completion | **Graduation certificate** (毕业证书) — **no bachelor's degree certificate** ⚠ | **Graduation certificate + Bachelor's degree certificate** (学位证书) ✅ |
| Onward route | **Zhuanshengben (专升本)** — *"upwards from a zhuanke programme to a benke programme"* ✅ | Master's |

> ⚠⚠ **This is where most misinterpretation of Chinese credentials happens.** A Chinese student may say *"I graduated from university"* or *"I have a college diploma"* while holding a **3-year zhuanke graduation certificate and no degree**. Conversely, a **benke** graduate holds **two documents**. The AI must ask:
> 1. **How many years was the programme?** (2–3 → zhuanke; 4–5 → benke)
> 2. **Do you have a degree certificate (学位证书) as well as a graduation certificate (毕业证书)?**
>
> These two questions resolve nearly all Chinese higher-education ambiguity.

## 2.3 ⚠ Named as an open question by credential-evaluation literature

A source surfaced in this research is titled *"Associate's or (Incomplete) Bachelor's Degree?"* (Scholaro/IEI, on 3-year programmes) — indicating that **how to characterise a Chinese 3-year zhuanke is a live, contested question among credential evaluators**, not a settled matter. ⚠

**The correct AI behaviour:** describe the zhuanke **in its own domestic terms** — a 2–3 year practically-oriented higher-education programme yielding a graduation certificate without a bachelor's degree — and **do not assert an equivalence** to any foreign qualification. Equivalency is out of scope and genuinely disputed.

## 5.1 The Zhongkao (中考)

| Item | Detail | Verification |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Full purpose | **Dual function** — *"the exam scores are used for both junior high school graduation and senior high school admissions"* | ✅ |
| Formal name | Senior High School Entrance Examination / Graduation Examination of Junior High School | ✅ |
| Age | *"typically around age 15"* | ✅ |
| Administration | **Locally / provincially administered**, not a single national paper | ✅ (described as a nationwide standardized test in one source, but administered at local level ⚠ — see conflict below) |

> ⚠ **A documented source tension:** one source describes the Zhongkao as *"a nationwide standardized test taken by students at the conclusion of China's nine-year compulsory junior high school education"*, while the same body of sources treats it as locally administered with local papers and local admission cut-offs. **The likely reconciliation:** the Zhongkao is a *nationally mandated stage* but with **locally set papers and locally set admission thresholds** — i.e. "nationwide" describes its universality, not a single common paper. ❌ *Confirm the administrative level of Zhongkao paper-setting at MOE / provincial education departments.*

> ⚠ **Why the Zhongkao matters for counselling:** it is a **high-stakes sorting examination** that determines whether a Chinese student enters **general academic senior high school** (the gaokao track) or **vocational senior secondary school** (a different track with different onward options). A Chinese student's answer to *"did you go to a 普通高中 (general high school) or a 中职 (vocational school)?"* is the single most informative question about their secondary pathway. ✅

❌ *Zhongkao subjects, scoring, and the share of students routed into the vocational track — not researched. The vocational-track share is policy-relevant in China and would be valuable context.*

## 6.2 Senior secondary graduation examinations — a two-name, two-era system ✅

**This is a genuinely confusing area and the sources are precise about it:**

| Examination | Chinese | Introduced | Purpose | Counts toward university admission? |
|-------------|---------|-----------|---------|-------------------------------------|
| **Huikao** (Joint Graduation Examination) | **会考** | **1993** ✅ | *"tests all subject areas of the curriculum"*; a **requirement for graduation** ✅ | **No** — *"is not a factor in higher education admissions"* ✅ |
| **Academic Proficiency Test (APT)** | **学业水平测试** (xuéyè shuǐpíng cèshì) | — | *"tests a smaller number of subjects"* ✅ | **Yes** — *"has, in accordance with reforms passed in 2014, been incorporated into higher education admissions alongside the gaokao"* ✅ |

> ✅ **AI rule:** if a Chinese student mentions a **huikao** result, it is a **graduation** examination and, historically, **not part of admission**. If they mention an **Academic Proficiency Test (学业水平测试)** result, it **may form part of admission** under the post-2014 reforms. **These are different examinations with different functions.** Ask which, and ask the province and year.

❌ *Which provinces use huikao vs the Academic Proficiency Test today, and how APT results are weighted in admission — requires further research at MOE / provincial authorities.* [PERIODIC]

# 7. The Gaokao (高考)

**China's National College Entrance Examination — the single most consequential examination in the Chinese system.**

| Item | Detail | Verification |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Chinese | **高考** (gāokǎo) | ✅ |
| Full name | National College Entrance Examination | ✅ |
| Level | End of senior secondary (Grade 12) | ✅ |
| Purpose | Admission to higher education (benke and zhuanke) | ✅ |
| Administration | **Provincial**, under national MOE policy | ✅ |
| Total score | Commonly **750 points** ⚠ | ⚠ secondary source; varies with the reform model and province |
| Timing | Early June ⚠ | ⚠ |

## 7.1 The two reformed subject models ✅

**China is mid-reform, with two models running in parallel:**

### The "3+3" model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The first "3" | **Three mandatory subjects** — Chinese, Mathematics, a Foreign Language ✅ |
| The second "3" | *"choose any three subjects from a menu of six: Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Politics, History, and Geography"* ✅ |
| Where | **6 provincial-level regions**, *"including Beijing, Shanghai and Shandong province"* ✅ |
| First adopters | **Shanghai and Zhejiang, in 2017** — *"the first two provincial-level regions in China to adopt the system"* ✅ |

### The "3+1+2" model

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The "3" | **Three mandatory** — Chinese, Mathematics, a Foreign Language ✅ |
| The "1" | *"choose either physics or history as their primary elective"* ✅ |
| The "2" | *"two of four other subjects: chemistry, biology, political ideology and geography"* ✅ |
| Where | **23 provinces** ✅ |
| Recent adopters | In **June 2022**, eight provinces/regions — **Sichuan, Henan, Shanxi, Shaanxi, Yunnan, Qinghai, Ningxia, Inner Mongolia** — announced implementation *"starting from 2025"* ✅ |

### Reform coverage

> *"By 2025, a total of 29 provinces across the country had implemented the new Gaokao, with 23 of them adopting the '3+1+2' mode."* ✅

## 7.2 The legacy model

⚠ Before the reforms, the gaokao operated on a **science stream / arts (humanities) stream** division (理科 / 文科), with a combined science paper (综合) or combined arts paper. ⚠ **This was not verified in this pass**, but it matters: a Chinese applicant who sat the gaokao **before their province's reform** took a **stream-based** examination, not a subject-choice one.

> ⚠ **AI rule:** a gaokao result must be read with **three facts**: the **province**, the **year**, and (post-reform) the **subject combination**. Without the province and year, the AI cannot know which model applied or what the cut-offs were. ❌ *Verified description of the pre-reform gaokao structure and the province-by-province reform commencement years — requires further research.*

## 7.3 Why a gaokao score is not nationally comparable

| Reason | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Provincial papers** | Not all provinces sit the same paper ⚠ |
| **Provincial cut-offs** | Admission thresholds ("批次线") are set per province ⚠ |
| **Provincial quotas** | Institutions allocate places by province ⚠ |
| **Different models** | 3+3, 3+1+2, and legacy stream-based scores are not the same measurement ✅ |
| **Hukou** | Where a student may sit the gaokao is tied to household registration, not simply residence ⚠ |

> ⚠ **The AI must not treat a gaokao score as a percentage or a portable measure.** It should record the score **with the province, year and model**, and treat it as **provincially referenced**. Some foreign universities do accept gaokao scores for direct entry, but that is an equivalency/admissions question, out of scope here.

| In force | **1 May 2022** ✅ |
| Length | **69 articles** ✅ |
| Headline change | *"the promulgation of the revised law marked the nation's formal recognition through legislation that vocational education holds an equally important status as general education and provided legal assurance for the transition of vocational education from level to type."* ✅ |
| Equal-opportunity clause | *"Students receiving vocational education shall enjoy equal opportunities for further education and employment as those receiving general education at the same level."* ✅ |
| Vocational undergraduate | *"Certain programs established by a higher vocational education institution providing junior college education to train high-end technicians and skilled workers which meet the conditions such as in-depth integration with industries, distinctive characteristics, and relatively high quality of training may be extended to undergraduate vocational education, with the approval of the education administrative department under the State Council."* ✅ |

> ✅ **The phrase to remember is "from level to type" (由层次到类型).** Chinese policy has formally reframed vocational education as a **parallel type** of education rather than a **lower level** of it. This is a substantive legal statement, and it is the basis for **职业本科 (vocational undergraduate)** degrees existing at all.
>
> ⚠ **But note the gap between law and practice.** The equal-status principle is a legal declaration; whether Chinese employers, universities and families treat vocational qualifications as equal is a separate, empirical question this document does not answer.

# 9. Post-Secondary / Higher Education

## 9.1 The Degrees Law (2024) — the current legal basis ✅

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Instrument | **Law of the People's Republic of China on Academic Degrees** ("Degrees Law") |
| Passed | **2024**, by the **NPC Standing Committee** ✅ |
| **In force** | **1 January 2025** ✅ |
| Replaces | The earlier **Regulations on Academic Degrees** (1980; *"last updated in 2004"*) ✅ |
| Degree tiers | *"adhering to the three-tier system of bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees"* ✅ |
| **Degree types** | *"degrees are divided into bachelor's degrees, master's degrees, and doctoral degrees, including **academic degrees, professional degrees, and other types**, and are conferred based on the categories of academic disciplines and professional degrees"* ✅ |
| Purpose | *"to regulate degree-granting activities, guarantee the quality of degrees, and protect the legitimate rights and interests of degree applicants"* ✅ |
| Direction | *"aims to strengthen both legal oversight and international engagement"*; *"includes stricter conditions for awarding degrees, focusing on quality control and accountability"* ✅ |

> ✅ **Two things the AI should take from the Degrees Law:**
> 1. China formally distinguishes **academic degrees (学术学位)** from **professional degrees (专业学位)** at master's and doctoral level. A Chinese "master's" may be either, and the distinction is legally recognised. **Ask which.**
> 2. The law took effect on **1 January 2025**, so degrees awarded before and after that date were conferred under **different legal regimes**. [STABLE going forward; historically relevant]

❌ *The full text's provisions on programme durations, credit requirements, and the academic/professional degree distinction in detail — not read in this pass. The MOE and `chinalawtranslate.com` (an unofficial translation) are available sources. **Priority gap.***

## 9.2 ⚠⚠ The dual-credential system — read this before interpreting any Chinese degree

**China issues two separate documents for higher-education completion:** ✅

| Document | Chinese | What it certifies |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| **Graduation certificate** | **毕业证书** | *"given when the required coursework is completed"* ✅ — i.e. **completion of the programme** |
| **Degree certificate** | **学位证书** | *"Separate academic degrees that are awarded based on academic achievement"* ✅ — i.e. **conferral of the degree** |

**Direct from the sources** ✅:

> *"China operates a dual credential system whereby students can earn both qualifications and degrees, which are verified separately as they serve different purposes in China. Both documents are essential for a complete academic record, and **it is possible to hold a graduation certificate without a degree certificate, and vice versa**."*

> *"There are two different certificates in most cases; graduation certificate and degree certificate. The graduation certificate (Zhuanke or Benke) is given when the required coursework is completed."*

> *"A Benke Certificate of Graduation is often translated as a Diploma or Graduation Certificate of a (4-year or 5-year) undergraduate course."*

**What this means in practice:**

| Situation | Documents held | Interpretation |
|-----------|----------------|----------------|
| Completed a **zhuanke** programme | Graduation certificate only ⚠ | Programme completed; **no bachelor's degree** |
| Completed a **benke** programme and met degree requirements | **Graduation certificate + Bachelor's degree certificate** ✅ | Full undergraduate degree |
| Completed a **benke** programme but did **not** meet degree requirements | **Graduation certificate only** ✅ | Programme completed; **degree not conferred** — a real and consequential situation |
| Awarded a degree without the graduation certificate | Degree certificate only ✅ (*"and vice versa"*) | Possible; requires explanation ⚠ |

> ⚠⚠ **AI rule — non-negotiable for Chinese students:** always ask **"do you have both a graduation certificate (毕业证书) and a degree certificate (学位证书)?"** A student with only a graduation certificate from a 4-year programme has **completed the programme but not been awarded the degree** — a materially different position from a full degree holder, and one that a single question resolves.
>
> ⚠ Note the translation trap: a **"Benke Certificate of Graduation"** is *"often translated as a Diploma"* ✅ — so a Chinese applicant may present a document translated as "Diploma" that actually represents **completion of a 4–5 year undergraduate programme**. **"Diploma" in a translated Chinese credential does not mean a sub-degree qualification.** This is the reverse of the trap in South Asian systems.

## 9.4 ⚠ Adult / continuing / self-study higher education — a flagged gap

China operates several **non-standard routes** to higher-education qualifications alongside the regular full-time route: adult higher education (成人高等教育), the self-study examination system (自学考试 / 自考), online/distance education (网络教育), and the Open University of China. ⚠

### ✅ RESOLVED — the three certificate categories and the four adult routes

**Research update (2026-08-21): previously logged as a significant unresearched area. Now established, including the key fact that the certificate itself distinguishes the route.**

**China issues THREE categories of Higher Education Qualification Certificate (HEQC)** ⚠:

| # | Category | Route |
|--:|----------|-------|
| 1 | **Regular higher education certificate** | Full-time, gaokao-entry (普通高等教育) |
| 2 | **Adult higher education certificate** | *"Its admission examination is uniformly organized by provincial and municipal education departments and its graduates receive nationally recognized unified adult education certificates"* (成人高等教育) |
| 3 | **Self-taught examination certificate** | The self-study examination route (自考) |

> ⚠⚠ **THE OPERATIVE FACT:** *"The different study modes are **indicated on the certificates themselves**, allowing institutions and employers to distinguish between regular, adult, and self-taught education pathways."* ⚠
>
> **So the AI does not have to guess** — a Chinese higher-education certificate states which of the three routes produced it. **The AI should ask the student to read the certificate's own wording**, and should recognise that all three are CHSI-verifiable but are treated differently by Chinese employers and by foreign institutions.

**The four forms of adult continuing education** ⚠ — *"There are four major forms of adult continuing education: the **adult university entrance exam**, **self-taught higher education examinations**, **TV university modern open distance education** and **online education**, each of which has its own characteristics."*

**The self-taught examination (自考) in detail** ⚠:
- *"a national academic examination for self-learners"*
- *"an **entirely open enrollment policy** that accepts applications from all backgrounds and academic levels"*
- Applicants *"register with the **provincial Self-taught Higher Education Examinations Committee** and complete the program **at their own pace**"*

> ⚠ **Counselling relevance:** the 自考 route has **no entry requirement at all** and no fixed duration. A 自考 bachelor's therefore carries a materially different selection signal from a gaokao-entry benke degree — while still being a genuine, nationally recognised, CHSI-verifiable Chinese qualification. **The AI should describe the route neutrally and accurately, not rank it.**

❌ *Remaining: the relative volumes of each route, and how foreign institutions typically treat 自考 and adult-education certificates. CHSI publishes English pages on this (`chsi.com.cn/en/aboutus/01.shtml` and `chsi.com.cn/en/news/201203/20120315/290797198.html`) — see the access note in §13.2.*

## 10.1 ⚠ China does not have a single unified national qualifications framework of the kind Nepal, India and Bangladesh have

**This is a genuine and important structural finding.** In place of a single NQF-style level framework, China's qualification architecture is defined by:

| Instrument | What it defines | Verification |
|------------|-----------------|--------------|
| **The Degrees Law (2024)** | The **three-tier degree system** (bachelor's / master's / doctoral) and the academic/professional/other degree types | ✅ |
| **The Vocational Education Law (2022)** | Vocational education as a **parallel type** rather than a lower level; the vocational undergraduate route | ✅ |

| **The discipline catalogue** | Degrees *"conferred based on the categories of academic disciplines and professional degrees"* ✅ — i.e. a national catalogue of disciplines and professional-degree categories governs what a degree is *in* | ✅ |

❌ **A definitive answer on whether China has adopted a national qualifications framework with numbered levels — not established in this pass.** *China has participated in international NQF-referencing discussions, and a "national vocational qualification" system has existed in various forms. **This is a priority research gap**, because the absence of a level framework is itself an important fact the AI should be able to state confidently rather than by inference.*

| **80–89** | **良好** | Good | ⚠ |

| **below 60** | **不及格** | Fail | ⚠ |

**The pass mark:** *"The 100-point scale uses **60** as the minimum passing score at **almost all** Chinese universities."* ⚠

> ⚠ Note the wording *"almost all"* — the 60% pass mark is near-universal but **not a legally mandated national standard**, and the descriptor boundaries are conventions rather than a national regulation. ⚠

## 11.2 ⚠⚠ There is no national GPA scale in China

**Direct from the source** ⚠:

> *"The Ministry of Education does not mandate a single national GPA scale, which means that Chinese universities have adopted slightly different conversion conventions. The exact conversion boundaries vary between institutions — some use 85% as the 'A' threshold while others use 90%."*

Named examples of institutions with their **own** conversion tables: **Fudan University, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Zhejiang University**. ⚠

> ⚠⚠ **This is the single most important grading fact for China, and it is a negative fact:** there is **no official Chinese GPA**, and **no official percentage→GPA conversion**. Any 4.0-scale GPA on a Chinese transcript comes from **that university's own** conversion table.

## 11.3 ⚠ Non-official conversions the AI must NOT use as authoritative

A widely circulated third-party mapping (90–100% → 3.7–4.0; 80–89% → 3.0–3.6; 70–79% → 2.3–2.9; 60–69% → 1.5–2.2) appeared in this research. ⚠

> ⚠⚠ **This is a third-party conversion, not an official Chinese standard, and the AI must not present it as one.** It is recorded here only so the AI recognises it when a student quotes such a figure and can say where it came from.
>
> **The correct answers when a conversion is needed are:**
> 1. The **GPA the Chinese university itself computes and prints** on the transcript (many do), or
> 2. The **destination institution's or evaluator's** own published methodology.
>
> ❌ *Any officially sanctioned Chinese percentage→GPA conversion — **not found. Treat as non-existent.***

# 14. Credit System

**Research update (2026-08-21): partially resolved. China does specify credit-hours nationally — but at the level of per-discipline teaching standards, not as a single cross-system formula.**

| Finding | Detail | Verification |
|---------|--------|--------------|
| **National teaching quality standards DO specify credit-hours** | China's **Ministry of Education** announced *"national teaching quality standards for bachelor's degree programmes"* on **30 January 2018**, covering **92 broad subject areas encompassing 587 disciplines**. They include *"Specifications for the knowledge and skills that should be taught on the programme, along with **credit-hours, degrees awarded and other requirements**"* | ✅ (British Council) |
| **Typical bachelor's credit range** | Per MOE statistics (2023), *"the standard four-year academic system accounts for **87.5% of all national undergraduate programs**, and students must complete **160–180 credits** to graduate"* | ⚠ |
| **Credit-to-hours conversion** | **Institution-specific.** Example: *"One credit equals to one class hour (45 mins) per week for one semester about 18 weeks"* at Xiamen University ⚠ | ⚠ |
| **What a credit represents** | *"One credit unit requires students to have workload of a certain number of hours spent on face-to-face lectures, major-related researches, surveys, experiments, graduation projects, internship and etc."* | ⚠ |
| **MOE position** | *"Chinese Ministry of Education encourages the employment of credit system"* — i.e. it is **encouraged, not mandated uniformly** | ⚠ |

> ⚠ **The corrected statement:** it is **wrong** to say China has no national credit specification. It is **right** to say China has **no single national credit-to-notional-hours formula** comparable to India's 30 hours or Bangladesh's 40 hours. Instead:
> - **Credit-hours are specified per discipline** in the MOE's 2018 national teaching quality standards (587 disciplines) ✅
> - **The hours-per-credit conversion is set by each institution** (~45 minutes × ~18 weeks is one reported convention) ⚠
> - **A Chinese bachelor's typically requires 160–180 credits** ⚠ — a much higher count than a US 120-credit or Indian 120-credit degree, because the unit is smaller
>
> ⚠⚠ **This makes the cross-country credit warning sharper, not weaker.** A Chinese **160–180 credit** bachelor's, a US **120 credit** bachelor's, a UK **360 credit** bachelor's and an Indian **120 credit** bachelor's are all first degrees. **The numbers differ by a factor of three in both directions because the units are entirely different.** Never compare them.

❌ *Remaining: the MOE's 2018 national teaching quality standards themselves (587 disciplines, each with credit-hour specifications) — not obtained. And whether any national hours-per-credit rule exists. Reduced from a total blocker to a partially-answered gap.*

> ⚠ **Contrast with India and Bangladesh**, both of which publish explicit credit arithmetic (India: 1 credit = 30 notional hours, 40 credits/year; Bangladesh: 1 credit = 40 notional hours for lecture-based learning). **No comparable Chinese national definition was found.**
>
> ❌ *Whether China has a national credit definition or credit minima per qualification level, and how credits are defined in practice at Chinese universities — **priority research gap**. Until resolved, the AI must not assume a Chinese credit is equivalent to any other country's credit, and should report credit counts as printed on the transcript without interpretation.*

❌ *Credit transfer, recognition of prior learning, and whether China operates a credit-bank mechanism — not researched.*

| Where a student sits it | Tied to **hukou** (household registration) ⚠ |
| Volume-based allocation | Applicants are admitted into **benke** or **zhuanke** tiers according to score bands ⚠ |
| Other routes | Independent recruitment, sports/arts specialist admission, and policies for minority and disadvantaged applicants ⚠ ❌ *not researched* |

> ✅ **The subject-choice fact is genuinely useful for counselling:** under the **"3+1+2"** model, the **physics-vs-history** choice at Grade 10 effectively determines the student's programme options three years later. A Chinese student who chose **history** may be **ineligible for engineering programmes domestically**. The AI should ask which primary elective they took.

❌ *Chinese domestic admission mechanics — application timing, preference/choice systems, tier bands, and the postgraduate entrance examination (考研) — **priority gap.***

# 19. Academic Pathways

**Main academic pathway:**
```text
Kindergarten (幼儿园, optional)
  → Primary (小学), Grades 1–6                    ┐ nine-year compulsory,
  → Junior secondary (初中), Grades 7–9           ┘ free
       → ZHONGKAO (中考)
  → General senior high (普通高中), 3 years
       → Huikao / Academic Proficiency Test  (graduation requirement)
       → Senior High School Graduation Diploma (普通高中毕业证书)
       → GAOKAO (高考)
  → BENKE (本科), 4–5 years
       → graduation certificate (毕业证书) + bachelor's degree certificate (学位证书)
  → MASTER'S (硕士), academic or professional
  → DOCTORAL (博士), academic or professional
```

**Zhuanke pathway:**
```text
Gaokao (lower score band) OR vocational entry
  → ZHUANKE (专科) / higher vocational college (高职), 2–3 years
       → graduation certificate only (no bachelor's degree)
       → employment
       → ZHUANSHENGBEN (专升本) → benke programme → bachelor's degree ⚠
```

**Vocational pathway:**
```text
Zhongkao
  → Secondary vocational school (中职: 中专 / 技校 / 职业高中), 3 years
       → employment
       → higher vocational college (高职 = zhuanke level)
              → VOCATIONAL UNDERGRADUATE (职业本科) — benke level, per the 2022 Law ✅
       → general higher education, under the 2022 Law's equal-opportunity clause ✅ (mechanism ⚠)
```

**Non-standard higher-education routes** (⚠ unresearched but real):
```text
Adult higher education (成人高等教育) · self-study examination (自考) ·
online/distance education (网络教育) · Open University of China
  → CHSI-verifiable qualifications whose certificates indicate the study mode ⚠
```

**International-qualification-in-China pathway:**
```text
IB / A-Level / AP / foreign provincial curriculum taken in China
  → interpreted under that curriculum's own framework, NOT the Chinese system
```

# 21. International Recognition Context (descriptive only)

**What exactly is a "Chinese senior high school qualification"?**

- The **Senior High School Graduation Diploma (普通高中毕业证书)**, awarded on completion of **three years of general senior high school (普通高中)** following **nine years of compulsory education** (6 primary + 3 junior secondary) — a total of **12 years of schooling**. ✅
- Graduation requires passing the senior-secondary graduation examination — the **huikao (会考)** or the **Academic Proficiency Test (学业水平测试)** depending on province and era. ✅
- **It is not the university-entrance mechanism.** Chinese university admission runs on the **gaokao (高考)**, a separate, **provincially administered** examination. ✅
- Since 2014 reforms, **Academic Proficiency Test** results are *"incorporated into higher education admissions alongside the gaokao"*, while **huikao** results are **not**. ✅
- The diploma is **independently verifiable** through **CSSD/CHSI**, as are provincial examination results. ✅
- A student who took the **vocational** senior-secondary track (中职) holds a **secondary vocational graduation certificate**, not a 普通高中毕业证书 — a different credential from a different track. ⚠

**What exactly is a "Chinese bachelor's degree"?**
- A **benke (本科)** qualification from a **4–5 year** programme, evidenced by **two documents**: a **graduation certificate (毕业证书)** and a **bachelor's degree certificate (学位证书, 学士)**. Under the **Degrees Law (in force 1 January 2025)**, degrees are conferred *"based on the categories of academic disciplines and professional degrees"*. Both documents are separately verifiable through CHSI. ✅

**What exactly is a "Chinese zhuanke"?**
- A **2–3 year**, *"more practically-oriented"* higher-education programme, delivered largely by **higher vocational colleges (高职)**, yielding a **graduation certificate but no bachelor's degree**. Holders may progress to a benke via **专升本 (zhuanshengben)**. ✅ / ⚠
- ⚠ **How to characterise it against foreign qualifications is genuinely contested** among credential evaluators (§2.3). This document deliberately does not assert an equivalence.

**What exactly is a "Chinese vocational undergraduate (职业本科)"?**
- A **benke-level** qualification delivered by a higher vocational institution whose programme was *"extended to undergraduate vocational education, with the approval of the education administrative department under the State Council"*, under the **2022 Vocational Education Law**. It exists because Chinese law now treats vocational education as a **parallel type, not a lower level**. ✅

*Cross-country equivalency is deliberately out of scope for this document.*

# 24. China-Specific Complexity Summary

The six things that most often go wrong when interpreting a Chinese student's education:

1. **Two documents, not one** — graduation certificate (毕业证书) **and** degree certificate (学位证书), verified separately, and either can be missing. (§9.2)
2. **Zhuanke is not a bachelor's degree** — 2–3 years, graduation certificate only, but students may call it "university" or "college". (§2.2)
3. **"Diploma" in a translated Chinese credential may mean a 4–5 year benke graduation certificate** — the opposite of the South Asian "diploma" meaning. (§9.2)
4. **The gaokao is provincial** — score, subject model, paper and cut-offs all vary by province and year. Four facts are needed, not one. (§7.3)
5. **No national GPA scale** — any GPA on a Chinese transcript is that university's own conversion. Do not convert. (§11.2)
6. **Huikao ≠ Academic Proficiency Test** — one is a graduation requirement that doesn't count for admission; the other counts for admission. (§6.2)

Plus: **vocational education is now legally a parallel type, not a lower level**, and **职业本科 vocational undergraduate degrees exist** (§8.1); **CHSI/CSSD is the single official verification channel** (§13.2); and **China has no numbered national qualifications framework** of the kind Nepal, Bangladesh, Australia and the UK use (§10.1).
