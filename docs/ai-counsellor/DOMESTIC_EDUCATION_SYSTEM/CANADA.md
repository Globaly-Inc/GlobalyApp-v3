> ## ⚠ THE FIVE THINGS THAT MOST OFTEN GO WRONG WITH CANADA
>
> 1. **Canada has NO federal department of education.** Education is *"the exclusive jurisdiction of provinces and territories"* ✅ — **13 separate systems** (10 provinces + 3 territories). There is no Canadian national curriculum, no national school-leaving examination, and no national grading scale.
> 2. **Québec is structurally different from the rest of Canada.** Secondary school ends at **Secondary V**, which *"corresponds to grade 11 in other jurisdictions"* ✅, followed by **CEGEP** (2 years pre-university or 3 years technical). A Québec bachelor's degree is therefore typically **3 years**, not 4.
> 3. **Canada's own official framework openly acknowledges this.** The CDQF says bachelor's instruction is *"typically six to eight semesters or more in duration (normally 90-120 credits, or the equivalent)"* — explicitly *"Owing primarily to variations in pre-university studies among the provinces/territories"*. ✅
> 4. **Canadian MD, DDS and JD/LLB are classified as bachelor's-level programs.** *"Though considered to be bachelor's programs in academic standing, some professional programs yield degrees with other nomenclature."* ✅
> 5. **Canadian university GPA scales are not standard** — 4.0, 4.3/4.33, 9-point, 12-point and percentage systems all exist, varying by province **and by institution**. ⚠

# 2. Education Levels — Actual Structure

**Canada has two structurally different patterns: the Québec pattern and everywhere else.**

## 2.1 Outside Québec (the "12+4" pattern)

```text
KINDERGARTEN (and often Junior Kindergarten) — jurisdiction-dependent ⚠
        ↓
ELEMENTARY / PRIMARY  — Grades 1–6 (or 1–8, jurisdiction-dependent) ⚠
        ↓
MIDDLE / JUNIOR HIGH  — where it exists, Grades 7–9 ⚠
        ↓
SECONDARY / HIGH SCHOOL — to Grade 12
        └── PROVINCIAL SECONDARY SCHOOL DIPLOMA
              Ontario: OSSD (30 credits, OSSLT, 40 hrs community involvement) ✅
              BC: Certificate of Graduation / "Dogwood Diploma" (80 credits + provincial
                  numeracy and literacy assessments) ✅
              Alberta: Alberta High School Diploma (100 credits, mainly "30"-level) ⚠
              (and one per other jurisdiction)
        ↓                                         ↓
UNIVERSITY                              COLLEGE / POLYTECHNIC / INSTITUTE
├── Bachelor's (typically 4 yr,         ├── Certificate ⚠
│    90–120 credits) ✅                  ├── Diploma ⚠
├── Master's (2–6 semesters) ✅          ├── Advanced Diploma ⚠
└── Doctoral (3–6 years) ✅              └── Applied/Bachelor's degrees at some colleges ⚠
```

## 2.2 Québec (the "11 + CEGEP" pattern) ✅

```text
KINDERGARTEN
        ↓
ELEMENTARY  — 6 years  ✅ ("Elementary education is spread over six years")
        ↓
SECONDARY   — 5 years, Secondary I to Secondary V, ages ~12–17 ✅
              (Secondary I = "grade 7" in other jurisdictions)
        └── DES — Diploma of Secondary Studies (Diplôme d'études secondaires) ✅
              "Students graduate high school after Secondary 5 (corresponds to
               grade 11 in other jurisdictions)" ✅
        ↓
CEGEP (Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel) ✅
        "CEGEP encompasses grade 12 from high school and the first year of university" ✅
        ├── PRE-UNIVERSITY programs — 2 years → DEC / DCS ✅
        └── TECHNICAL programs — generally 3 years → DEC / DCS in a specific field ✅
              "Technical training at the college level provides access to the job market
               or to university, so long as the university prerequisites are met" ✅
        ↓
UNIVERSITY — bachelor's typically 3 years after a DEC ⚠
        (this is the "90 credits" / "six semesters" end of the CDQF range ✅)
```

> ⚠⚠ **Three consequences of the Québec structure the AI must handle:**
> 1. A **Québec DES represents 11 years of schooling**, not 12. It is **not** directly comparable to an Ontario OSSD or a BC Dogwood Diploma in years.
> 2. A **CEGEP DEC** is a genuine post-secondary qualification that covers *"grade 12 from high school and the first year of university"* ✅ — so a DEC holder has completed **13 years** (pre-university stream) or **14 years** (technical stream) of education.
> 3. A **Québec 3-year bachelor's degree is a complete bachelor's degree**, not a shortened one — because the missing year was done at CEGEP. The CDQF explicitly attributes its 90–120 credit range to *"variations in pre-university studies among the provinces/territories"*. ✅
>
> **AI rule:** if a Canadian student's degree was 3 years, **ask whether they studied in Québec and whether they hold a DEC.** If yes, the 3-year degree is normal and complete.

# 6. CEGEP — Québec's distinctive post-secondary level

**CEGEP has no counterpart anywhere else in Canada, and understanding it is essential.**

| Item | Detail | Verification |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Full name | **Collège d'enseignement général et professionnel** | ✅ |
| What it spans | *"CEGEP encompasses grade 12 from high school and the first year of university"* | ✅ |
| Entry | The **DES** (Québec secondary diploma) ✅ |
| **Pre-university stream** | *"Pre-university training bridges the gap between secondary and university studies. It leads to a Diploma of College Studies (DCS) and lasts **two years**"* | ✅ |
| **Technical stream** | *"Technical training at the college level provides access to the job market or to university, so long as the university prerequisites are met. This training leads to a Diploma of College Studies (DCS) in a specific field and generally lasts **three years**"* | ✅ |
| Award | **DEC** — *"All students completing CEGEP earn a diploma called a DEC (Diplôme d'études collégiales)"*; in English, **DCS (Diploma of College Studies)** | ✅ |
| Onward | University (typically a **3-year** bachelor's) ⚠, or employment (technical stream) ✅ |

> ✅ **The CDQF explicitly recognises the CEGEP diploma as a university-entry qualification:** bachelor's admission *"normally requires, at a minimum, a **secondary school or CEGEP diploma** and/or university preparatory courses"*. ✅ This is Canada's own framework naming CEGEP alongside secondary school as an entry route.

> ⚠ **Counselling note:** a **technical DEC** is a **three-year post-secondary technical qualification** that provides *"access to the job market **or** to university"* ✅ — i.e. it is simultaneously a terminal vocational credential and a university pathway. That dual character makes it structurally unlike most three-year diplomas elsewhere.

❌ *CEGEP grading (the "cote R" / R-score used in Québec university admission), the number of CEGEPs, and public/private CEGEP provision — not researched. **The R-score is a priority gap**, because it is Québec's university-admission metric.*

# 8. The Canadian Degree Qualifications Framework (CDQF)

**Canada's nearest equivalent to an NQF — but note carefully what it is and is not.**

| Item | Detail | Verification |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Official name | **Canadian Degree Qualifications Framework (CDQF)** | ✅ |
| Instrument | Endorsed by the **Ministerial Statement on Quality Assurance of Degree Education in Canada, 2007** | ✅ |
| Endorsed by | *"All provinces and territories in Canada have endorsed the Ministerial Statement … through the Council of Ministers of Education, Canada (CMEC)"* | ✅ |
| Companion documents | *"Procedures and Standards for New Degree Program Quality Assessment"* and *"Procedures and Standards for Assessing New Degree-Granting Institutions"* | ✅ |
| **Purpose** | *"to provide assurance to the public, students, employers, and postsecondary institutions that new programs and institutions meet appropriate standards, and to provide a context for identifying **how degree credentials compare in level and standard to those in other jurisdictions**"* | ✅ |
| **Scope** | **Degrees only** — Bachelor's, Master's, Doctoral. It defines *"the degree categories … their typical length, minimum admission requirements and qualification standards"* | ✅ |
| Standards | *"degree level expectations" (DLEs)* — *"describe competencies and general learning outcomes"* | ✅ |
| **Cumulative** | *"intended to be cumulative — each degree level presupposes the accomplishment of an earlier one"* | ✅ |

## 8.1 ⚠ What the CDQF is NOT

> ⚠⚠ **The CDQF is a degree framework, not a national qualifications framework.** It covers **only bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees**. It does **not** number levels 1–10, and it does **not** cover:
> - secondary school diplomas
> - college/polytechnic certificates, diplomas and advanced diplomas
> - CEGEP DECs
> - apprenticeship and trade qualifications
>
> **So there is no single PAN-CANADIAN ladder placing a college diploma relative to a bachelor's degree**, in the way the AQF (Australia), BNQF (Bangladesh) or NQF (Nepal) do. This is a genuine structural difference and the AI should state it rather than inventing a national level.

### ✅ RESOLVED (partially) — but PROVINCIAL frameworks exist: the Ontario Qualifications Framework

**Research update (2026-08-21): the earlier statement that Canada has nothing covering sub-degree qualifications was too strong. Ontario has a full 13-level qualifications framework.**

**The Ontario Qualifications Framework (OQF)** ✅ — `ontario.ca/page/ontario-qualifications-framework`:

| OQF level | Qualification |
|----------:|---------------|
| **1** | Certificate I — entry-level career college certificates |
| **2** | Certificate II — career college and CAAT certificates |
| **3** | **Certificate of Apprenticeship** |
| **4** | **Certificate of Qualification** (trade qualification) |
| **5** | Certificate III — **Ontario College Certificate** (CAAT) |
| **6** | Diploma I — career college diplomas |
| **7** | Diploma II — **Ontario College Diploma** (CAAT) |
| **8** | **Ontario College Advanced Diploma** |
| **9** | Post-Diploma Certificate — **Ontario College Graduate Certificate** |
| **10** | **Baccalaureate/Bachelor's Degree** |
| **11** | **Baccalaureate/Bachelor's Degree: Honours** |
| **12** | **Master's Degree** |
| **13** | **Doctoral Degree** |

**Scope:** *"all non-religious postsecondary certificate, diploma and degree programs"* — including apprenticeship certificates, career college qualifications, public college (CAAT) credentials, and university degrees from both public institutions and those authorised by ministerial consent. ✅

> ✅ **Three things this changes:**
> 1. **Canada does have numbered qualification frameworks — at the provincial level.** The correct statement is: *there is no pan-Canadian framework covering sub-degree qualifications, but at least one province (Ontario) operates a 13-level framework that does.*
> 2. **The OQF places apprenticeship and trade credentials on the same ladder as degrees** (levels 3–4 alongside college and university credentials) — something the CDQF does not do.
> 3. **The OQF distinguishes Bachelor's (level 10) from Bachelor's Honours (level 11)** as separate levels — mirroring Australia's AQF 7/8 split rather than the UK's single FHEQ 6.
>
> ⚠ **But note the trap:** OQF levels are **Ontario's own numbering** and are **not comparable with the AQF, SCQF, RQF, BNQF, NHEQF or NCrF**. An "OQF level 8 Advanced Diploma" is not an "AQF level 8 Bachelor Honours Degree". **The AI must always name the framework alongside the level.**

❌ *Whether the other 12 jurisdictions operate comparable frameworks — **not established**. Ontario's is confirmed; the rest remain unresearched. Reduced from a blocker to a coverage gap.*

## 8.2 The six dimensions of the degree-level standards ✅

> *"The standards stipulate the demonstrable transferable learning skills and level of mastery of a body of specialized knowledge in six dimensions: **1. Depth and Breadth of Knowledge, 2. Knowledge of Methodologies, 3. Application of Knowledge, 4. Communication Skills, 5. Awareness of Limits of Knowledge, and 6. Professional Capacity/Autonomy.** The shades of distinction between degrees are determined by the capacity of the graduate at each level to act competently, creatively and independently, and by their proximity to the forefront of a discipline and/or profession."* ✅

## 8.3 Bachelor's degree — CDQF ✅

| Item | CDQF text |
|------|-----------|
| **Length** | *"Owing primarily to **variations in pre-university studies among the provinces/territories**, classroom instruction is typically **six to eight semesters or more** in duration (normally **90-120 credits, or the equivalent**) and may be supplemented by required professional experience (e.g., supervised practica, internships, and work terms)."* |
| **Admission** | *"Admission normally requires, at a minimum, a **secondary school or CEGEP diploma and/or university preparatory courses, a minimum grade-point average**, and other program-specific requirements. Students lacking these credentials may be admitted on a part-time or probationary basis, with continuation subject to acceptable academic achievement."* |
| **Second-entry programs** | *"normally require at least **two or three years of completed degree-level studies** or in some cases the prior or concurrent completion of another undergraduate degree."* |

**The four types of bachelor's program the CDQF names** ✅:

| Type | Description (CDQF) | Examples given |
|------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Broad education** | *"designed to provide a broad education as an end in itself. They may also prepare graduates for employment in a variety of fields and/or for admission to second-entry professional programs."* | **BHum (Humanities), General BA and General BSc degrees** |
| **In-depth disciplinary** | *"designed to provide in-depth study in academic disciplines. They normally prepare students for graduate study in the discipline(s) and for employment in a variety of fields."* | — |
| **Applied focus** | *"blend theory and practice, with content selected to ensure mastery of the field of practice rather than to deepen knowledge in the discipline… graduates may or may not require preparatory studies before entering graduate programs."* | — |
| **Professional focus** | *"designed to prepare graduates to meet admission requirements and to be competent practitioners in the profession. Some of them are first-entry programs, others are second-entry programs… They normally require periods of practical experience (apprenticeship, internship, articling, clinical, etc.)"* | **DDS (Dental Surgery), MD (Medicine), LLB, or JD (Juris Doctor)** |

> ✅⚠ **THE CRITICAL SENTENCE:** *"**Though considered to be bachelor's programs in academic standing**, some professional programs yield degrees with other nomenclature."* — with **MD, DDS, LLB and JD** given as the examples. ✅
>
> **This means a Canadian MD, DDS, LLB or JD is, in Canada's own framework, a bachelor's-level program** despite carrying "Doctor" in the name. This is one of the highest-value single facts in this entire research project, and it is stated in Canada's own official framework document.
>
> ⚠ Note also that these are typically **second-entry** programs requiring *"at least two or three years of completed degree-level studies"* or a prior degree ✅ — so an MD holder has done substantially more than 4 years of higher education, even though the MD is bachelor's-level in academic standing.

## 8.4 Master's degree — CDQF ✅

| Item | CDQF text |
|------|-----------|
| **Length** | *"Master's programs vary typically from **two to six semesters** in duration, depending on the field and the speed at which individuals progress through requirements."* |
| **Admission** | *"Normally, an undergraduate degree with an appropriate specialization or an undergraduate degree with relevant bridging studies."* |
| Nature | *"builds on knowledge and [greater] autonomy than a bachelor's-degree program. Much of the study undertaken at the master's level will have been at, or [near, the forefront of] application of knowledge, and they will understand how [to] deal with complex issues both systematically [and creatively]"* (extraction partial ⚠) |
| **Research-oriented master's** | *"typically for [students who need] the research, analytical, methodological, interpretive, and expository skills necessary for doctoral studies or for [equivalent work]"* |
| **Profession-oriented master's** | *"normally admit students [preparing for] a particular profession or field of practice or, if they are already involved in the profession or field, to extend their knowledge base and skills as professionals/practitioners."* Example given: **MSW (Social Work)** |
| Preparation for | *"Graduates will have the qualities needed for either further study in the discipline or for employment in circumstances requiring sound judgment, personal responsibility and initiative, in complex and unpredictable professional environments."* |

> ⚠ **"Two to six semesters" is a wide range — roughly 1 to 3 years.** A Canadian master's may be a **1-year** (two-semester) coursework degree or a **2–3 year** research degree. The AI must ask the duration rather than assume. ✅

## 8.5 Doctoral degree — CDQF ✅

| Item | CDQF text |
|------|-----------|
| **Length** | *"A doctoral program is typically **three to six years** in length, depending on the field and the speed at which individuals progress through requirements."* |
| **Admission** | *"Normally, a master's degree with an appropriate specialization or a master's degree with appropriate bridging studies."* |
| Nature | *"builds on the knowledge [acquired at the] graduate level. Study at the doctoral level is at the forefront of an academic or professional [field]."* Holders *"must have autonomy, an ability to conceptualize, design, [and] interpret knowledge that extends the forefront of [the field]"* |
| Course work | *"Preparation for doctoral work may involve course work of varying lengths aimed at [ensuring mastery] of the discipline prior to authorization to [proceed]"* |
| **Research-oriented doctoral** | *"focus on [an original] contribution to knowledge in the form of a [dissertation] without diluting the significance of the dissertation as the primary demonstration of mastery."* Leads to the **PhD**. Examples: **PhD (Psychology), PhD (Education), PhD (Music)** |
| **Practice-oriented doctoral** | *"of a more applied nature, relate to a professional or creative activity and, where there is an internship or exhibition requirement, may also require a dissertation. Doctoral programs with an orientation to practice typically involve more course work than doctoral programs with a more theoretical or disciplinary focus."* Examples: **EdD (Education), MusDoc (Music), PsyD (Psychology)** |
| Preparation for | *"employment requiring the ability to make informed judgements on complex issues in specialist fields, and innovation in tackling and solving problems"* |

*(⚠ Bracketed text above marks places where the PDF's multi-column layout produced partial extraction; the quoted fragments are verbatim but some sentences are incomplete. Read the CDQF PDF directly for full wording.)*

> ✅ **Note the EdD / MusDoc / PsyD classification:** Canada distinguishes **research doctorates (PhD)** from **practice-oriented doctorates (EdD, PsyD, MusDoc)** — both at doctoral level, with the practice-oriented ones typically involving **more coursework**. ✅ This is a genuine doctoral-level distinction, unlike the MD/DDS/JD case (§8.3), which is **bachelor's-level despite the "Doctor" title**.

## 10.2 University GPA scales — the documented variation ⚠

| Scale | Where (as reported) |
|-------|--------------------|
| **4.0** | *"Ontario universities predominantly use a 4.0 scale"* ⚠ |
| **4.0 or 4.33** | *"Alberta uses a 4.0 or 4.33 scale depending on the institution"* ⚠ |

# 19. International Recognition Context (descriptive only)

**What exactly is a "Canadian secondary school diploma"?**

- **One of 13 jurisdiction-specific credentials.** There is no single Canadian high school diploma. ✅
- **Outside Québec**, it represents **12 years of schooling**, with graduation requirements set per province — e.g. **Ontario's OSSD** (30 credits, the OSSLT literacy requirement, and 40 hours of community involvement) ✅; **BC's Certificate of Graduation / "Dogwood Diploma"** (80 credits including 52 required, plus provincial numeracy and literacy assessments) ✅; **Alberta's High School Diploma** (100 credits, mainly at the "30" level) ⚠.
- **In Québec**, the **DES** represents **11 years** of schooling and *"corresponds to grade 11 in other jurisdictions"* ✅. Québec students then complete **CEGEP** before university.
- **The credit totals are not comparable across provinces** — 30, 80 and 100 credits each represent a complete diploma. ✅
- Canada has **no national school-leaving examination**; the diploma, not an exam result, is the credential. ✅ / ⚠

**What exactly is a "CEGEP DEC"?**
- A **Diploma of College Studies (Diplôme d'études collégiales)** from Québec's CEGEP system, which *"encompasses grade 12 from high school and the first year of university"* ✅. Two forms: **pre-university (2 years)** and **technical (3 years)**. ✅ Canada's own CDQF names the **CEGEP diploma** alongside the secondary school diploma as a bachelor's-entry qualification. ✅

**What exactly is a "Canadian bachelor's degree"?**
- A degree whose *"classroom instruction is typically **six to eight semesters or more** in duration (normally **90-120 credits**, or the equivalent)"* ✅ — with the range attributed by the CDQF itself to *"variations in pre-university studies among the provinces/territories"* ✅. A **Québec 3-year (90-credit) bachelor's is a complete degree**, because its holder completed CEGEP first. ⚠
- It may be a **broad-education, in-depth disciplinary, applied, or professional** program. ✅

**What exactly is a Canadian "MD", "DDS", "JD" or "LLB"?**
- ✅ **In Canada's own framework, a bachelor's-level program**: *"Though considered to be bachelor's programs in academic standing, some professional programs yield degrees with other nomenclature."* ✅ They are typically **second-entry**, requiring *"at least two or three years of completed degree-level studies"* or a prior degree ✅.

**What exactly is a Canadian "EdD", "PsyD" or "MusDoc"?**
- ✅ A **practice-oriented doctoral degree** — genuinely doctoral level, *"of a more applied nature"*, typically involving *"more course work than doctoral programs with a more theoretical or disciplinary focus"*, and where there is an internship or exhibition requirement *"may also require a dissertation"*. ✅

**What exactly is a "Canadian master's degree"?**
- A degree of *"typically two to six semesters"* ✅ — so roughly **1 to 3 years** — in either a **research-oriented** or **profession-oriented** form. ✅ Admission normally requires *"an undergraduate degree with an appropriate specialization or an undergraduate degree with relevant bridging studies."* ✅

*Cross-country equivalency is deliberately out of scope for this document.*

# 22. Canada-Specific Complexity Summary

The six things that most often go wrong when interpreting a Canadian student's education:

1. **There is no Canadian education system — there are 13.** "Which province or territory?" is the first question. (§1.2)
2. **Québec is structurally different**: 11 years of school (DES) + CEGEP (DEC) + a 3-year bachelor's. A 3-year Québec degree is complete. (§2.2, §6)
3. **Canadian secondary "credits" are not comparable across provinces** — 30 (ON), 80 (BC) and 100 (AB) each mean a full diploma. (§5.3)
4. **MD, DDS, JD and LLB are bachelor's-level programs in Canada's own framework** despite the "Doctor" nomenclature — while EdD, PsyD and MusDoc are genuinely doctoral. (§8.3, §8.5)
5. **University GPA scales vary by institution** — 4.0, 4.3, 4.33, 9-point, 12-point and percentage all exist. Always ask the maximum. (§10.2)
6. **The CDQF covers only degrees.** Canada has no single framework placing college diplomas, CEGEP DECs or trade credentials relative to degrees. Do not invent a level. (§8.1)

Plus: **an ECA is an immigration document, not an admission requirement** (§11); **"college" in Canada means a career-focused post-secondary institution** (§20); and **Ontario's OSSD compulsory-credit split changed in 2024-25** (§5.2).
