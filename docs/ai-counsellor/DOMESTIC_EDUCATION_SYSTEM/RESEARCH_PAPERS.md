## ⚠ Read this first — an honest assessment of coverage

**Peer-reviewed academic literature is thin across all eight countries, and that is only partly a defect of this research.**

Three things happened consistently:

1. **The searches surfaced official documents, not journal articles.** Queries about education structure, qualification frameworks, grading and credit systems returned **ministry documents, regulator pages, framework PDFs and national-recognition-body guides**. For this project's purpose — *what does a qualification mean inside its own system* — those sources are **more authoritative and more current** than academic literature. A journal article about Indian higher-education policy is less useful than the UGC's NHEQF; a paper on Australian VET is less useful than training.gov.au.

2. **The most valuable "papers" turned out to be credential-evaluation literature**, not education research: WES/WENR, Nuffic, IQAS Alberta, TAICEP, AACRAO EDGE, APNNIC, UK ENIC and CICIC. These bodies exist to answer exactly this project's question, and several publish detailed public country guides. **They are catalogued in [SOURCE_DIRECTORY.md](SOURCE_DIRECTORY.md), not here, because they are Tier 1/3 institutional sources rather than research papers.**

3. **A systematic literature search was not performed for any country.** What follows is what surfaced incidentally during structural research. **No claim is made that this is a representative or complete survey of the literature.**

**Bangladesh has no academic literature listed at all.** That is a genuine gap, not a judgement about the field.

### Bibliographic honesty

Several entries below carry `[to confirm]` in the author, year or journal field. **This means the field could not be established from the search result and was deliberately left blank rather than guessed.** No citation in this file is invented — every title, author, journal and URL was actually surfaced. Where a DOI was available it is given; where it was not, no DOI is fabricated.

# Credential-evaluation literature — the most directly applicable body of work

**This is the class of source GlobalyApp should mine hardest in a follow-up pass.** These organisations exist to answer *"what does this foreign qualification mean?"*, and several publish free public country guides. They are catalogued in [SOURCE_DIRECTORY.md](SOURCE_DIRECTORY.md) under Tier 3.

| **CICIC** (Canada) | Canada's National Information Centre under UNESCO conventions; the Directory of Educational Institutions in Canada | 🇨🇦 |
| **WES / WENR** | *World Education News + Reviews* — practitioner analysis; **the source for the China huikao vs Academic Proficiency Test distinction** | 🇨🇳, 🇮🇳 |

| **TAICEP** | Professional-association guidance — Chinese degrees history; **vocational-education evaluation tips** | 🇨🇳 |
| **AACRAO EDGE** | Country credential profiles (subscription) | 🇧🇩, 🇨🇳 |
| **APNNIC** | The Asia-Pacific analogue of ENIC-NARIC — qualification-system and school-system profiles | 🇦🇺 (and the wider region) |

For GlobalyApp these are among the most directly usable sources available for any country in this research.
